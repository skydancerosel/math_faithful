## 7. The path space and the fundamental theorem

This chapter proves Theorems 17.1 and 17.3 and builds the finite-dimensional model of Milnor's §16, which Theorem 22.1 and Bott periodicity also use. Throughout, $N \subseteq V$ is a slice submanifold, $p, q \in V$, $\rho$ the distance [V7.18], $\Omega = \Omega(N; p, q)$ [V8.1], $E = E_0^1$ the energy [V8.5], $\Omega^a = \{E \le a\}$, and a *geodesic* of $\Omega$ is an unbroken geodesic [V8.10], with the index and nullity of $E_{**}$ [V8.14, V9.13]. Theorem 16.3, Corollaries 17.2, 17.4 and 17.5, the spheres, Milnor's map $h$, and Examples 2–3 and the Corollary of the Appendix are not needed.

### 7.1 Milnor's metric

Paths are maps $\mathbb R \to V$ of which only $[0, 1]$ matters; $\dot s_\omega = \|\omega'\|$ is the speed.

**Proposition (Milnor's metric)** [V10.1]. For $\omega, \eta \in \Omega$,
$$d(\omega, \eta) = \sup_{0 \le t \le 1}\rho(\omega(t), \eta(t)) + \Big(\int_0^1(\dot s_\omega - \dot s_\eta)^2\,dt\Big)^{1/2}$$
is an extended pseudometric on $\Omega$, and paths that agree on $[0, 1]$ are at distance $0$. ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.instPseudoEMetricSpace⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Omega · PathSp.edist_eq_zero_of_eqOn⟧

*Proof.* The triangle inequality pointwise, resp. Minkowski's (velocities are continuous on closed pieces); both terms see only $[0, 1]$. $\square$

From now on $\Omega$ carries the topology of $d$. It is not Hausdorff, but a map agreeing on $[0, 1]$ with a path of $\Omega$ lies in $\Omega$, and a map into $\Omega$ at distance $0$ from a continuous map is continuous ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · PathSp.continuous_of_edist_eq_zero⟧.

**Lemma 16.A (the energy is continuous)** [V10.2]. For $0 \le a \le b \le 1$, $|\sqrt{E_a^b(\omega)} - \sqrt{E_a^b(\eta)}| \le (\int_0^1(\dot s_\omega - \dot s_\eta)^2)^{1/2}$. So $\sqrt{E_a^b}$ is $1$-Lipschitz for $d$ and $E_a^b$ is continuous on $\Omega$. ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.abs_sqrt_energy_sub_le⟧ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.continuous_energy⟧

*Proof.* $\sqrt{E_a^b} = \|\dot s\|_{L^2[a, b]}$: Minkowski on $[a, b]$, then enlarge $[a, b]$ to $[0, 1]$. $\square$

### 7.2 Broken geodesics

**Definition (Milnor's data)** [V10.3]. Let $c > 0$ and $S = \{x \in N : \rho(p, x) \le \sqrt c\}$. *Milnor's data* are $p, q \in N$; $c$ with $S$ compact; $\delta > 0$ such that any $y, z \in S$ with $\rho(y, z) < \delta$ are joined as in Corollary 10.8 [V7.20] at the scale $\delta$ (a unique $v \in T_yN$ with $\|v\| < \delta$, $\exp_yv = z$; then $\|v\| = \rho(y, z)$, and $v$ depends smoothly on $(y, z)$), and $\delta$ is at most a normal radius [V7.9] at each point of $S$; and a subdivision $0 = t_0 < \dots < t_{m+1} = 1$ with $c\,\ell_i < \delta^2$, $\ell_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$. $B$ is the set of $\omega \in \Omega$ with $E(\omega) < c$ that are geodesics on each $(t_i, t_{i+1})$, and $B^a = B \cap \Omega^a$. ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup⟧ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenPaths · BrokenSetup.brokenPaths⟧

**Lemma (Milnor's choices)** [V10.3]. (i) If $E(\omega) \le c$, then $\omega([0, 1]) \subseteq S$. (ii) If $N$ is closed in $V$, $S$ is compact. (iii) For compact $K \subseteq N$ some $\delta > 0$ joins points of $K$ at distance $< \delta$ as in Corollary 10.8 at the scale $\delta$ and is at most a normal radius on $K$. (iv) If $p, q \in N$ and $S$ is compact, there is $\eta > 0$ such that every subdivision with all $\ell_i < \eta$ is part of Milnor's data; some uniform one is. ⟦Chapter10/Broken · isCompact_closedRiemBall_of_isClosed⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · corollary_10_8'⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · exists_brokenSetup_uniform⟧

*Proof.* (i) $\rho(p, \omega(t))^2 \le (L_0^t)^2 \le tE_0^t \le c$ [V8.5]. (ii) $S$ is closed and $\|x - p\| \le \rho(p, x)$ [V7.21]. (iii) Cover $K$ by finitely many neighbourhoods of the local Corollary 10.8 and Lemma 10.3; take the least scale. (iv) $\eta = \delta^2/c$. $\square$

Fix Milnor's data. For $y \in V^m$ put $x_0 = p$, $x_j = y_j$, $x_{m+1} = q$, $\rho_i = \rho(x_i, x_{i+1})$,
$$E'(y) = \sum_{i=0}^m\frac{\rho_i^2}{\ell_i}, \qquad U = \{y \in N^m : \text{all } \rho_i < \infty,\ E'(y) < c\},$$
and let $v(x, x')$ be the vector of Corollary 10.8 from $x$ to $x'$ ($0$ if there is none).

**Lemma 16.1** [V10.4]. (i) $U = N^m \cap O$ for an open $O \subseteq V^m$; for every open $O$, $N^m \cap O$ is a slice submanifold of $V^m$ modelled on $Z^m$, with tangent space $\prod_jT_{y_j}N$ at $y$. (ii) For $y \in U$ the *broken geodesic* $\omega_y$, equal on $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$ to $\tau \mapsto \exp_{x_i}(\frac{\tau - t_i}{\ell_i}v(x_i, x_{i+1}))$, lies in $B$, has break points $(\omega_y(t_1), \dots, \omega_y(t_m)) = y$, and $E(\omega_y) = E'(y)$. (iii) For $\omega \in B$ the break points $y$ of $\omega$ lie in $U$, and $\omega_y = \omega$ on $[0, 1]$. ⟦Chapter10/BrokenPaths · BrokenSetup.lemma_16_1⟧ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenEnergy · BrokenSetup.exists_isOpen_nodes⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Product · tangentSubspace_pi_inter⟧

*Proof.* (ii) For $y \in U$, $\rho_i^2 \le \ell_iE'(y) < c\,\ell_i < \delta^2$, and by Cauchy–Schwarz $\rho(p, x_k)^2 \le (\sum_i\rho_i)^2 \le (\sum_i\ell_i)E'(y) < c$: the vertices lie in $S$ and consecutive ones are joined. Each segment is a geodesic of constant speed $\rho_i/\ell_i$, whence the energy. (iii) A piecewise smooth path that is a geodesic on $(t_i, t_{i+1})$ is smooth on $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$, hence the radial geodesic $\exp_{\omega(t_i)}(\frac{\tau - t_i}{\ell_i}w)$ of its initial velocity [V9.9], with $\|w\|^2 = \ell_iE_{t_i}^{t_{i+1}}(\omega) < \delta^2$ and $\rho_i \le \|w\|$ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenPaths · BrokenSetup.exists_segVec⟧; uniqueness in Corollary 10.8 gives $w = v(\omega(t_i), \omega(t_{i+1}))$, and $E' \le E(\omega) < c$. (i) The gaps $(\rho_i)_i \in [0, \infty]^{m+1}$ are continuous on $N^m$ [V7.21]; products of slice charts, restricted to open sets, are slice charts. $\square$

### 7.3 The energy on the manifold of break points

Write $U^a = \{y \in U : E'(y) \le a\}$.

**Theorem 16.2: smoothness and compactness** [V10.6]. (i) Every $y_0 \in U$ has an open neighbourhood $O \subseteq V^m$ and a function smooth on $O$ equal to $E'$ on $U \cap O$; so $E'$ is continuous on $U$. (ii) For $a < c$, $U^a$ is compact. ⟦Chapter10/BrokenEnergy · BrokenSetup.exists_smooth_energy'⟧ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenEnergy · BrokenSetup.isCompact_sublevel⟧

*Proof.* (i) $\rho$ is not smooth on the diagonal, but $\rho_i^2 = \|v(x_i, x_{i+1})\|^2$ and, near $y_0$, uniqueness identifies $v$ with the smooth map of Corollary 10.8. (ii) $U^a \subseteq S^m$. If $\rho(p, q) = \infty$, $U = \emptyset$; otherwise the gaps are finite on $S^m$, $E'$ is continuous there, and $U^a = S^m \cap \{E' \le a\}$. $\square$

**Theorem 16.2: critical points** [V10.9]. Let $O \subseteq V^m$ be open with $N^m \cap O \subseteq U$, and $F : V^m \to \mathbb R$ smooth with $F = E'$ on $N^m \cap O$. For $y \in N^m \cap O$ and $w \in \prod_jT_{y_j}N$, $dF_y(w) = -2\sum_j\langle w_j, \Delta_{t_j}\omega_y'\rangle$; and $y$ is a critical point of $F|(N^m \cap O)$ [V1.4] if and only if $\omega_y$ is a geodesic. Every geodesic $\gamma \in \Omega$ with $E(\gamma) < c$ has its break points $y$ in $U$, with $\omega_y = \gamma$ on $[0, 1]$, $\omega_y$ a geodesic and $E'(y) = E(\gamma)$. ⟦Chapter10/BrokenVariation · BrokenSetup.fderiv_eq_jumps⟧ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenVariation · BrokenSetup.isCriticalPointM_iff⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Finite · BrokenSetup.eqOn_path_nodeVal_of_isUnbrokenGeodesic⟧

*Proof.* Take a smooth curve $y(u)$ in $N^m \cap O$ with velocity $w$. Then $u \mapsto \omega_{y(u)}$ is a variation [V8.2], smooth on each strip since a geodesic segment depends smoothly on its initial data over the whole segment [V9.9], with variation field $y'(0)_j$ at $t_j$ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenVariation · BrokenSetup.exists_variation_path⟧. The acceleration vanishes on the segments, so the first variation [V8.7] at $\omega_y$ is $-\sum_j\langle X(t_j), \Delta_{t_j}\omega_y'\rangle$, and $F(y(u)) = E(\omega_{y(u)})$ gives the formula. At a critical point test with the tangent vector $w = (\Delta_{t_j}\omega_y')_j$: all jumps vanish, and Corollary 12.3 [V8.11] applies; conversely a geodesic has no jumps. A geodesic is broken for every subdivision; apply Lemma 16.1 (iii). $\square$

**Lemma 15.4 for the subdivision of §16** [V10.10]. Let $y \in U$ with $\gamma = \omega_y$ a geodesic. (i) If $J$ is a Jacobi field along $\gamma$ and $0 \le a$, $0 < h$, $a + h \le 1$, then $s \mapsto J(hs + a)$ is a Jacobi field along $s \mapsto \gamma(hs + a)$. (ii) $t_i$ and $t_{i+1}$ are not conjugate along $\gamma$ [V9.4]. (iii) $W \mapsto (W(t_j))_j$ is a linear bijection from the broken Jacobi fields along $\gamma$ with break points $t_1, \dots, t_m$ [V9.17] onto $\prod_jT_{y_j}N$. (iv) $E'(\omega(t_1), \dots, \omega(t_m)) \le E(\omega)$ for all $\omega \in \Omega$; hence $E_{**}(W, W) \ge 0$ for $W \in T\Omega_\gamma$ vanishing at the $t_j$, and $> 0$ if moreover $W \ne 0$ vanishes off $[0, 1]$. (v) Among the fields vanishing off $[0, 1]$, the broken Jacobi fields and those vanishing at the $t_j$ are complementary; the index of $E_{**}$ is that of its restriction to the broken Jacobi fields, whose null space has dimension the nullity of $E_{**}$. ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.brokenNodeMap_bijective⟧ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.formIndex_brokenSpace⟧ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.finrank_ker_brokenSpace⟧

*Proof.* (i) The second covariant derivative and the curvature term scale by $h^2$. (ii) The rescaled segment is $s \mapsto \exp_{x_i}(sv_i)$ with $\|v_i\| < \delta \le$ a normal radius; Remark 14.5 [V9.11] and uniqueness for the Jacobi equation. (iii) Injective by (ii); surjective since by (ii) each segment carries a Jacobi field with given end values, and these glue. (iv) On each segment $\rho(\omega(t_i), \omega(t_{i+1}))^2 \le \ell_iE_{t_i}^{t_{i+1}}(\omega)$. So along $u \mapsto \exp_{\gamma(t)}(uW(t))$, which fixes the break points, the energy is $\ge E'(y) = E(\gamma)$; its second derivative at $0$, $E_{**}(W, W)$ by Remark 13.5 [V8.17], is $\ge 0$ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · nonneg_of_isLocalMin_of_hasDerivAt⟧. By the second variation formula broken Jacobi fields are $E_{**}$-orthogonal to fields vanishing at the $t_j$; so if $E_{**}(W, W) = 0$, Cauchy–Schwarz for the semidefinite form makes $W$ a null vector, a Jacobi field [V9.5] vanishing at $t_0$ and $t_1$, hence $0$ by (ii). (v) $X = X_b + (X - X_b)$, $X_b$ the broken Jacobi field matching $X$ at the $t_j$; the abstract Lemma 15.B [V9.14] gives the index. A null vector of the restriction has no projected jumps (test it against the broken Jacobi field with values $P\Delta_{t_j}X'$), so it is a Jacobi field vanishing at $0$ and $1$, and conversely; these form a space of dimension the nullity [V9.6]. $\square$

> **Hidden hypothesis.** Milnor quotes Lemma 15.4 [V9.19], proved for subdivisions fine in the sense of §15, which the subdivision of §16 need not be; (iv)–(v) use the normal radius and the minimality of broken geodesics instead.

### 7.4 The deformation of $\Omega^a$ onto $B^a$

**Lemma 16.C (Milnor's deformation $r_u$)** [V10.7]. For $\omega \in \Omega$ with $E(\omega) < c$, $y$ its break points, and $t_i \le u \le t_{i+1}$, let $r_u(\omega)$ be $\omega_y$ on $[0, t_i]$, the short geodesic $\tau \mapsto \exp_{\omega(t_i)}(\frac{\tau - t_i}{u - t_i}v(\omega(t_i), \omega(u)))$ on $[t_i, u]$, and $\omega$ on $[u, 1]$; the formulas of neighbouring segments agree at $u = t_i$. On $[0, 1]$: $r_u(\omega) \in \Omega$, $E(r_u\omega) \le E(\omega)$, $r_0\omega = \omega$, $r_1\omega = \omega_y$, and $r_u\omega = \omega$ if $\omega \in B$. Let $\bar r_u\omega$ be $r_u\omega$ on $[0, 1]$ and $\omega$ off it, and $\bar r_u\omega = \omega$ if $u \notin [0, 1]$ or $E(\omega) \ge c$. Then $(u, \omega) \mapsto \bar r_u\omega$, $\mathbb R \times \Omega \to \Omega$, is continuous on $[0, 1] \times \{E < c\}$. ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.retr⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.continuousOn_deform⟧

*Proof.* $\rho(\omega(t_i), \omega(u))^2 \le (u - t_i)E(\omega) < \delta^2$, so the short geodesic exists; Schwarz's inequality on each segment bounds the energy; a sub-arc of a short geodesic is the short geodesic between its ends, so $r_u$ fixes $B$. For continuity let $u_j \to u$ in one segment, $\omega_j \to \omega$ (any filter); the segments glue by the agreement at the $t_i$. *Uniform part:* paths of energy $\le c$ satisfy $\rho(\omega(s), \omega(\tau)) \le (c|\tau - s|)^{1/2}$, so pointwise convergence, from the continuity of short geodesics in their ends [V7.20, V7.23], is uniform. *$L^2$ part:* on $[0, t_i]$ the speeds $\rho_k/\ell_k$ converge; beyond $\max(u_j, u)$ they are those of $\omega_j$, $\omega$; in between, a short geodesic over a short interval may be fast, but its energy $\rho(\omega_j(t_i), \omega_j(u_j))^2/(u_j - t_i) \le E_{t_i}^{u_j}(\omega_j)$ is small by *Lemma 16.B*: if $\omega_j \to \omega$, for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is $\eta > 0$ with eventually $E_a^b(\omega_j) < \varepsilon$ whenever $b - a < \eta$ (Lemma 16.A and a bound on the speed of $\omega$) ⟦Chapter10/RetractionCont · PathSp.eventually_energy_lt⟧ ⟦Chapter10/RetractionConv · BrokenSetup.tendsto_retrSeg⟧. $\square$

**Theorem 16.2: deformation retraction** [V10.8]. $\bar r_0 = \mathrm{id}$; $E(\bar r_u\omega) \le E(\omega)$; $\bar r_1\omega \in B$ if $E(\omega) < c$; $\bar r_u\omega = \omega$ for $\omega \in B$, $u \in [0, 1]$. So for $a < c$, $B^a$ is a strong deformation retract of $\Omega^a$, and $\bar r_1 : \Omega^a \to B^a$ and the inclusion are inverse homotopy equivalences. ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.deform_one_mem_brokenPaths⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.homotopyEquivBroken⟧

*Proof.* [V10.7] and Lemma 16.1 (ii). $\square$

**Proposition ($B^a$ and $U^a$)** [V10.5]. $y \mapsto \omega_y$ is continuous from $U$ to $\Omega$, $\omega \mapsto (\omega(t_j))_j$ from $\Omega$ to $V^m$, and for every $a$ they restrict to inverse homotopy equivalences between $B^a$ and $U^a$. ⟦Chapter10/Omega · BrokenSetup.continuous_pathP⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Omega · BrokenSetup.homotopyEquivNodes⟧

*Proof.* The uniform part as in [V10.7]; the speeds $\rho_k(y)/\ell_k$ are continuous on $U$; $\|x - y\| \le \rho(x, y)$. One composite is the identity (Lemma 16.1 (ii)); the other agrees with the identity on $[0, 1]$ (Lemma 16.1 (iii)), so the homotopy equal to it for $t < 1$ and to the identity at $t = 1$ is at distance $0$ from a projection, hence continuous. $\square$

### 7.5 Telescopes (Milnor's Appendix)

Spaces are arbitrary; $X_0 \subseteq X_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq X$.

**Fact (homotopy equivalences)** [VA.1] (standard). $f$ is a homotopy equivalence if $gf \simeq \mathrm{id}$, $fg \simeq \mathrm{id}$ for some $g$. Composites, maps homotopic to one, homotopy inverses, and maps with a left and a right homotopy inverse (the Assertion of p. 22 [V2.14]) are homotopy equivalences. ⟦Appendix/Telescope · IsHomotopyEquiv⟧ ⟦Appendix/Telescope · IsHomotopyEquiv.of_leftInv_rightInv⟧

**Fact (telescopes)** [VA.2, VA.3] (standard). $X_\Sigma = \bigcup_nX_n \times [n, n + 1] \subseteq X \times \mathbb R$, and $X$ is the *homotopy direct limit* of $\{X_n\}$ if $p(x, \tau) = x$ is a homotopy equivalence $X_\Sigma \to X$. $(x, \tau) \in X_\Sigma$ iff $x \in X_n$ for an integer $n \le \tau$. If the $X_n$ are closed, a function on $P \times X_\Sigma$ continuous on each $P \times X_n \times [n, n + 1]$ is continuous. ⟦Appendix/Telescope · IsHomotopyDirectLimit⟧ ⟦Appendix/Telescope · continuous_of_stripMap⟧

**Fact (maps of telescopes; truncations)** [VA.4, VA.5] (standard). If $f$ maps each $X_n$ into $Y_n$, $f_\Sigma(x, \tau) = (f(x), \tau)$ satisfies $pf_\Sigma = fp$; between homotopy direct limits, $f$ is a homotopy equivalence if $f_\Sigma$ is. The truncations $X^n_\Sigma = \{\tau \le n\}$ are closed, and $x \mapsto (x, n)$ is a homotopy equivalence $X_n \to X^n_\Sigma$. ⟦Appendix/Telescope · IsHomotopyEquiv.of_telescopeMap⟧ ⟦Appendix/Telescope · isHomotopyEquiv_truncIncl⟧

**Fact (a criterion; Example 1)** [VA.6, VA.7] (standard). (i) If $f : X \to \mathbb R$ is continuous, $f \ge 0$, and $f \ge n + 1$ off $X_n$, then $X$ is the homotopy direct limit of $\{X_n\}$: $(x, (1 - s)f(x) + s\tau)$ deforms $X_\Sigma$ onto the graph of $f$. So $X_\Sigma$ is that of its truncations ($f = \tau + 1$). (ii) If $X$ is paracompact Hausdorff and each point lies in the interior of some $X_n$, take $f = \sum_nn\varphi_n$, $(\varphi_n)$ a partition of unity. (iii) If $E : X \to \mathbb R$ is continuous and $a_n \uparrow \infty$, $X$ is the homotopy direct limit of $\{E \le a_n\}$, for any $X$: take $f = \sum_j\min(1, \max(0, E - a_j + 1))$. ⟦Appendix/Examples · example1⟧ ⟦Appendix/Examples · isHomotopyDirectLimit_sublevel⟧

**Fact (Theorem A, Milnor's proof)** [VA.9, VA.12] (standard). Let $X$, $Y$ be the homotopy direct limits of increasing $\{X_n\}$, $\{Y_n\}$, the $Y_n$ closed, and let $f : X \to Y$ map each $X_n$ into $Y_n$ by a homotopy equivalence. Then $f$ is a homotopy equivalence. ⟦Appendix/TheoremA · theoremA⟧

*Proof idea.* **Case 1** [VA.10]: for closed $X_n$ and $\varphi|X_n \simeq \mathrm{id}$, Milnor's homotopy $h_u$ (its third line is $h^{n+1}_{(4t-3)u}$, misprinted $(4 - 3t)u$) deforms $\varphi_\Sigma$ to a map $\psi$ with $\psi(x, n + t) = (x, n + 2t)$ for $t \le \frac12$ and level $n + 1$ for $t \ge \frac12$, and every such $\psi$ is a homotopy equivalence ⟦Appendix/TheoremA · isHomotopyEquiv_of_milnorMap⟧. **Case 2** [VA.11]: Milnor's $G$, pasted on the strips of the $Y_n$ (this needs them closed), makes $Gf_\Sigma$ and $f_\Sigma G$ preserve the closed truncations and restrict to maps homotopic to the identity at integer levels; Case 1 on the truncations, [VA.6] and the Assertion make $f_\Sigma$ a homotopy equivalence ⟦Appendix/TheoremA · theoremA_telescope⟧; then [VA.4]. $\square$

**Fact (mapping telescopes)** [VA.13, VA.14] (standard). For maps $K_0 \xrightarrow{c_0} K_1 \to \cdots$, $\operatorname{Tel}(K) = \bigsqcup_nK_n \times [0, 1]/(k, 1)_n \sim (c_nk, 0)_{n+1}$. Maps out of it (and homotopies) are given block by block with no closedness; it is the homotopy direct limit of its closed truncations $\{\text{level} \le n\}$, and $k \mapsto [k, 0]_n$ is a homotopy equivalence from $K_n$ to the $n$-th one. The mapping telescope $T_X$ of the inclusions $X_n \subseteq X_{n+1}$ maps to $X_\Sigma$ by a homotopy equivalence, and to $X$ by one if $X$ is the homotopy direct limit. ⟦Appendix/MappingTelescope · MappingTelescope.isHomotopyEquiv_levelIncl⟧ ⟦Appendix/MonotoneUnion · isHomotopyEquiv_telescopeProj'⟧
