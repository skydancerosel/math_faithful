## Part VIII. Broken geodesics (Milnor §16)

Milnor's §16 makes the path space finite dimensional. Below a fixed energy $c$, a path from $p$ to $q$ is straightened segment by segment into a *broken geodesic* with prescribed break times; the broken geodesics form a manifold $B$ of dimension $n(k-1)$, parametrized by their break points; the energy restricted to $B$ is a smooth function $E'$ with compact sublevel sets, whose critical points are exactly the geodesics from $p$ to $q$, with the index and nullity of $E_{**}$; and the straightening is a deformation retraction of $\Omega^a$ onto $B^a$. Hence, when $p$ and $q$ are not conjugate along the geodesics in $\Omega^a$, $\Omega^a$ has the homotopy type of a finite cell complex with one cell of dimension $\lambda$ for each geodesic of index $\lambda$ (Theorem 16.3). This Part retells the formal proof of all of it: Lemma 16.1, every assertion of Theorem 16.2, Theorem 16.3 for $B^a$ and for $\Omega^a$, and the Remark on p. 92. The labels V10.1–V10.14 are those of the verified edition.

The setting is that of Parts VI and VII. Milnor's $M$ is replaced by a slice submanifold $N$, modelled on $Z$ with complement $W$ (§0.3), of a finite-dimensional real inner product space $V$, with the induced metric and its distance $\rho$, which takes values in $[0, \infty]$ ⟦Chapter7/Distance · riemDist⟧; every Riemannian manifold is of this kind by Nash's embedding theorem, which is quoted, not proved. Further, $p, q \in V$, and $\Omega = \Omega(N; p, q)$ is the set of maps $\mathbb R \to V$ that are piecewise smooth on $[0, 1]$, lie in $N$ there and run from $p$ to $q$ ⟦Chapter8/Energy · pathSpace⟧ (§0.8), with energy $E(\omega) = E_0^1(\omega) = \int_0^1 \|\omega'\|^2$ ⟦Chapter8/Energy · energy⟧. Milnor's completeness of $M$ enters only through the compactness of one ball, and neither connectedness nor Milnor's assumption $\Omega^c \ne \emptyset$ is used.

The formal route departs from Milnor's in five places. (1) The manifold $B$ is represented by its set $U$ of break points, an open subset of $N^m$ and hence a slice submanifold; no structure is transported to $B$. (2) Paths are maps on $\mathbb R$, so Milnor's metric is a pseudometric, and his homeomorphism between $B$ and $U$ becomes a homotopy equivalence, a homeomorphism up to paths at distance $0$. (3) Lemma 15.4 does not apply as proved in §15 and is proved anew for the subdivision of §16. (4) Theorem 16.3 is proved through a smooth extension of $E'$ and a version of Theorem 3.5 for compact sublevel sets, assuming that $a$ is not the energy of a geodesic. (5) The continuity of Milnor's deformation $r_u$, left to the reader in the book, occupies two files. The formal development proves Lemma 16.1, Theorem 16.2 on $B$ and Theorem 16.3 for $B^a$ before $\Omega$ carries any topology; the metric enters only for the deformation retraction and for the passage from $B^a$ to $\Omega^a$. This Part follows that order.

**Map.**

### VIII.1 Subdivisions, the ball $S$ and the choice of $\delta$

**Subdivisions.** A subdivision with $m$ interior points is a strictly increasing $t : \{0, \dots, m+1\} \to \mathbb R$ with $t_0 = 0$ and $t_{m+1} = 1$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision⟧; Milnor's $k$ is $m + 1$, the number of segments. The $i$-th segment is $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$, $0 \le i \le m$, of length $\ell_i = t_{i+1} - t_i > 0$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision.len⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision.len_pos⟧, and the lengths add up to $1$ by telescoping ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision.sum_len⟧. All $t_i$ lie in $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision.t_nonneg⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision.t_le_one⟧ and are ordered as their indices ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision.t_lt_iff⟧. The interior break points $t_1, \dots, t_m$ form a finite set ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision.breaks⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision.mem_breaks⟧ contained in $(0, 1)$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision.breaks_subset⟧. Every number $\tau$ is assigned a segment: the first $i$ with $\tau < t_{i+1}$, and the last segment if there is none ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision.segOf⟧. So $t_i \le \tau < t_{i+1}$ puts $\tau$ in segment $i$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision.segOf_eq⟧, $\tau \ge 1$ in the last ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision.segOf_of_one_le⟧, and every $\tau \in [0, 1]$ lies in the closed interval of its segment ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision.mem_Icc_segOf⟧. The convention is asymmetric: an interior break point $t_{i+1}$ belongs to segment $i + 1$, not $i$, which is why several proofs below check that two formulas agree at a division point. A closed interval $[a, b] \subseteq [0, 1]$, $a < b$, with no break point in its interior lies in one segment ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision.exists_seg_of_piece⟧: take the segment $i$ of $a$; if $b > t_{i+1}$, then $t_{i+1}$ is an interior break point in $(a, b)$, unless $a = t_{i+1}$, which is impossible because then $a$ would belong to segment $i + 1$. Subdivisions of every mesh exist: the uniform one, $t_i = i/(m + 1)$, has all lengths $1/(m+1)$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision.uniform⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · Subdivision.uniform_len⟧.

**Vertices.** For $y = (y_1, \dots, y_m) \in V^m$ the vertices of a broken path with break points $y$ are $x_0 = p$, $x_j = y_j$ for $1 \le j \le m$, and $x_{m+1} = q$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · vert⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · vert_zero⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · vert_inner⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · vert_last⟧. (The formal indices of $y$ start at $0$; this Part numbers them from $1$, so that $y_j$ sits at $t_j$.)

**The ball $S$.** Milnor's $S$ is $\{x \in N : \rho(p, x) \le \sqrt c\}$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · closedRiemBall⟧, a subset of $N$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · closedRiemBall_subset⟧.

**Step 1 (Milnor p. 89).** *If $\omega \in \Omega$ has $E(\omega) \le c$, then $\omega(t) \in S$ for every $t \in [0, 1]$* ⟦Chapter10/Broken · mem_closedRiemBall_of_energy_le⟧. The restriction of $\omega$ to $[0, t]$ is a piecewise smooth path in $N$ from $p$ to $\omega(t)$, so $\rho(p, \omega(t)) \le L_0^t(\omega)$ ⟦Chapter7/Distance · riemDist_le_pathLength⟧ ⟦Chapter7/Path · pathLength⟧. Schwarz's inequality gives $(L_0^t)^2 \le t\,E_0^t$ ⟦Chapter8/Energy · pathLength_sq_le_energy⟧, and $t\,E_0^t \le E_0^t \le E_0^1 \le c$, the middle inequality because the integral of the nonnegative $\|\omega'\|^2$ grows with the interval. Hence $L_0^t \le \sqrt c$.

**$S$ is compact.** Milnor uses that $M$ is complete; the formal proof offers two embedded forms of that hypothesis. If $p \notin N$, then $\rho(p, \cdot) = \infty$ ⟦Chapter7/Distance · riemDist_eq_top_of_notMem⟧ and $S$ is empty, so assume $p \in N$.

- *$N$ closed in $V$* ⟦Chapter10/Broken · isCompact_closedRiemBall_of_isClosed⟧. The function $\rho(p, \cdot)$ is continuous on $N$ ⟦Chapter7/Metric · continuousOn_riemDist_left⟧, so $S$ is closed in the closed set $N$, hence in $V$. Since $\|x - p\| \le \rho(p, x)$ ⟦Chapter7/Metric · ofReal_norm_sub_le_riemDist⟧, $S$ lies in the closed ball of radius $\sqrt c$ about $p$, a compact set.
- *$N$ preconnected and $(N, \rho)$ complete* ⟦Chapter10/Broken · isCompact_closedRiemBall_of_isRiemComplete⟧ ⟦Chapter7/Metric · IsRiemComplete⟧. The Hopf–Rinow theorem of Part VI says that closed $\rho$-bounded subsets of $N$ are compact ⟦Chapter7/HopfRinow · hopf_rinow_of_isRiemComplete⟧. Here $S$, as a subset of $N$, is closed by continuity and $\rho$-bounded by $2\sqrt c$ through the triangle inequality at $p$ ⟦Chapter7/Distance · riemDist_triangle⟧ ⟦Chapter7/Metric · IsRiemBounded⟧.

**Corollary 10.8 with normal radii.** Part VI packages the conclusion of Milnor's Corollary 10.8, for $y, z \in N$ and a bound $\delta$, as one predicate ⟦Chapter7/Distance · IsShortJoin⟧: there is $v \in T_yN$ with $\|v\| < \delta$, $\exp_y v = z$ ⟦Chapter7/Exp · exp⟧ and $\rho(y, z) = \|v\|$; $v$ is the only tangent vector at $y$ of norm $< \delta$ with $\exp_y v = z$; and $v$ depends smoothly on the end points: there are an open $O_2 \ni (y, z)$ in $V \times V$ and a map $G$, smooth on $O_2$, with $G(y, z) = v$, such that for $y', z' \in N$ with $(y', z') \in O_2$ and $\rho(y', z') < \delta$, $G(y', z')$ is tangent at $y'$, of norm $< \delta$, and $\exp_{y'} G(y', z') = z'$. Part VI proves it locally: around each $p \in N$ there are $s > 0$ and an open $\Omega \ni p$ such that the predicate holds for $y \in N \cap \Omega$, $\rho(y, z) < \delta \le s$ ⟦Chapter7/Distance · exists_isShortJoin⟧.

The §16 file adds a requirement that Milnor never mentions, at no cost: $\delta$ must also be at most a normal radius at every point of $S$, an $\varepsilon$ such that $\exp_x$ maps the open $\varepsilon$-ball of $T_xN$ diffeomorphically onto an open set ⟦Chapter7/NormalNbhd · IsNormalRadius⟧. In the local statement, $\Omega$ is shrunk into the open set $O$ of Lemma 10.3, on which a single $\varepsilon$ is a normal radius at every point ⟦Chapter7/NormalNbhd · lemma_10_3⟧, and $s$ is replaced by $\min(s, \varepsilon)$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · exists_isShortJoin'⟧. Then **Corollary 10.8 with normal radii** ⟦Chapter10/Broken · corollary_10_8'⟧: *for compact $K \subseteq N$ there is $\delta > 0$ such that any $y, z \in K$ with $\rho(y, z) < \delta$ satisfy the predicate at scale $\delta$, and every point of $K$ has a normal radius $\ge \delta$.* For empty $K$, $\delta = 1$ works vacuously. Otherwise the open sets $\Omega_x$, $x \in K$, cover $K$; a finite subcover is chosen ⟦Mathlib/Topology/Compactness/Compact · IsCompact.elim_finite_subcover⟧ and $\delta$ is the least of the corresponding $s_x$. A point $y \in K$ lies in some $\Omega_x$ of the subcover, so the joins from $y$ hold at the scale $\delta \le s_x$, and the normal radius at $y$ is $\varepsilon_x \ge s_x \ge \delta$. The normal radius is used only for Jacobi fields along the segments (VIII.6, VIII.7).

**Milnor's data.** All results of this Part are stated for a structure that collects Milnor's choices on p. 89 ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup⟧, which this book calls *Milnor's data*: a subdivision; end points $p, q \in N$; an energy bound $c > 0$ such that $S$ is compact; a number $\delta > 0$, Milnor's $\varepsilon$, such that any two points of $S$ at distance $< \delta$ satisfy the conclusion of Corollary 10.8 at scale $\delta$, and such that every point of $S$ has a normal radius $\ge \delta$; and the fineness condition $c\,\ell_i < \delta^2$ for every segment, Milnor's $t_i - t_{i-1} < \varepsilon^2/c$. Trivially $p \in S$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.p_mem_ball⟧.

**Sufficiently fine subdivisions (V10.3).** *If $p, q \in N$, $c > 0$ and $S$ is compact, there is $\eta > 0$ such that every subdivision with all $\ell_i < \eta$ is part of Milnor's data* ⟦Chapter10/Broken · exists_brokenSetup⟧. Take $\delta$ from Corollary 10.8 with normal radii for $K = S$ and $\eta = \delta^2/c$; then $c\,\ell_i < c \cdot \delta^2/c = \delta^2$. Some uniform subdivision qualifies, by choosing $m$ with $1/(m+1) < \eta$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · exists_brokenSetup_uniform⟧. This makes exact the quantifier of Lemma 16.1 over all sufficiently fine subdivisions, and by the compactness of $S$ above, Milnor's data exist for every $c > 0$ when $N$ is closed in $V$, or connected and complete.

**The short join vector.** For $x, x' \in V$, $v(x, x')$ is a tangent vector at $x$ of norm $< \delta$ with $\exp_x v = x'$, chosen when one exists, and $0$ otherwise ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.joinVec⟧. Tangency is expressed with the total tangent projection $P_x$, the orthogonal projection onto $T_xN$ for $x \in N$ and $0$ off $N$ ⟦Chapter8/Energy · prj⟧, for which $P_x v = v$ if and only if $v \in T_xN$ ⟦Chapter8/Energy · prj_eq_self_iff⟧. *For $x, x' \in S$ with $\rho(x, x') < \delta$, $v(x, x')$ is tangent, of norm $< \delta$, $\exp_x v(x, x') = x'$, $\rho(x, x') = \|v(x, x')\|$, and it is the only tangent vector of norm $< \delta$ that $\exp_x$ sends to $x'$* ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.joinVec_spec⟧. The data supply such a vector with all these properties, so the choice is made, and by uniqueness the chosen vector is that one. One more fact is needed because $\exp$ is a total function with a default value: $(x, v(x, x'))$ lies in the domain of $\exp$, the pairs from which a geodesic runs on a neighbourhood of $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter7/Exp · expDom⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.mem_expDom_joinVec⟧. Otherwise $\exp_x v = x$ by the default, so $x' = x$, $\rho(x, x') = 0$, $v = 0$; but $(x, 0)$ always lies in the domain ⟦Chapter7/Exp · zero_mem_expDom⟧. Only the domain fact guarantees that $s \mapsto \exp_x(s\,v)$ is a geodesic.

### VIII.2 Lemma 16.1: a broken geodesic is its break points

**Definitions.** The energy of the broken geodesic with break points $y$ is given by formula $(\ast)$ of the exposition,
$$
E'(y) = \sum_{i=0}^{m} \frac{\rho(x_i, x_{i+1})^2}{\ell_i},
$$
the distances being read as real numbers ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.energy'⟧. Milnor's open set of break points is ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.nodes⟧
$$
U = \{y \in N^m : \rho(x_i, x_{i+1}) < \infty \text{ for all } i, \ E'(y) < c\}.
$$
The finiteness clause is a formal necessity: $\rho$ is infinite between points that no path joins, and the real part of $\infty$ is $0$ in the formal conventions, so without the clause a tuple with an infinite gap would look like one of small energy. A path $\gamma$ is a *broken geodesic* for the subdivision when it is a geodesic ⟦Chapter7/Geodesic · IsGeodesicOn⟧ on every open segment $(t_i, t_{i+1})$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.IsBroken⟧, and Milnor's $B = \operatorname{Int}\Omega(t_0, \dots, t_k)^c$ is the set of broken geodesics $\gamma \in \Omega$ with $E(\gamma) < c$ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenPaths · BrokenSetup.brokenPaths⟧. Milnor asks for a geodesic on the closed segment; the open one suffices, because *a piecewise smooth path that is a geodesic on $(a, b)$ is smooth on $[a, b]$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenPaths · contDiffOn_Icc_of_isGeodesicOn⟧: at an interior point the geodesic is smooth, and at $a$ (or $b$) the path's own smooth piece to the right of $a$ (left of $b$) ⟦Chapter8/Piecewise · exists_piece_right⟧ ⟦Chapter8/Piecewise · exists_piece_left⟧ gives smoothness on a neighbourhood within $[a, b]$; local smoothness at every point of $[a, b]$ is smoothness on $[a, b]$. The break points of a path are $(\gamma(t_1), \dots, \gamma(t_m))$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.nodeVal⟧, and for $\gamma \in \Omega$ their vertices are the values of $\gamma$ at all division points, $x_k = \gamma(t_k)$ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenPaths · BrokenSetup.vert_nodeVal⟧.

**From break points to a path.** Two estimates make every $y \in U$ the break points of a broken geodesic.

- *Consecutive vertices are at distance $< \delta$* ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.riemDist_vert_lt⟧. The terms of $E'$ are nonnegative ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.energy'_nonneg_terms⟧, so each satisfies $\rho_i^2/\ell_i \le E'(y) < c$, and $\rho_i^2 < c\,\ell_i < \delta^2$ by fineness.
- *All vertices lie in $S$* ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.vert_mem_ball⟧, the exposition's Step 4, which Milnor leaves implicit. The triangle inequality along the chain of vertices, by induction on $k$, gives $\rho(p, x_k) \le \sum_i \rho_i$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · riemDist_le_sum_filter⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · riemDist_le_sum⟧, and Cauchy–Schwarz in Sedrakyan's form ⟦Mathlib/Algebra/Order/BigOperators/Ring/Finset · sq_sum_div_le_sum_sq_div⟧ gives
$$
\Big(\sum_i \rho_i\Big)^2 \le \Big(\sum_i \ell_i\Big)\Big(\sum_i \frac{\rho_i^2}{\ell_i}\Big) = E'(y) < c .
$$
The vertices lie in $N$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.vert_mem⟧.

So the join vector specification applies to every pair of consecutive vertices ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.joinVec_vert⟧. Write $v_i = v(x_i, x_{i+1})$. The $i$-th segment of the broken geodesic is
$$
\tau \mapsto \exp_{x_i}\Big(\frac{\tau - t_i}{\ell_i}\, v_i\Big)
$$
⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.segPath⟧, and the broken geodesic $\omega_y$ takes at $\tau$ the value of the segment of $\tau$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.path⟧. Its properties are checked one at a time.

1. The $i$-th segment starts at $x_i$, since $\exp_x 0 = x$ ⟦Chapter7/Exp · exp_zero⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.segPath_start⟧, and ends at $x_{i+1}$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.segPath_end⟧.
2. *Each segment is a geodesic on an open interval $(t_i - \eta, t_{i+1} + \eta)$, with velocity $v_i/\ell_i$ at $t_i$* ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.segPath_isGeodesicOn⟧. The ray $s \mapsto \exp_{x_i}(s v_i)$ is a geodesic on some $(-\delta', 1 + \delta')$ ⟦Chapter7/Exp · IsGeodesicFrom.isGeodesicOn_exp_smul⟧, which needs the domain fact above; an affine change of parameter $s = (\tau - t_i)/\ell_i$ preserves geodesics ⟦Chapter7/Geodesic · IsGeodesicOn.comp_mul_add⟧, and $\eta = \delta'\ell_i$. The velocity follows from the chain rule and the velocity $v$ of $s \mapsto \exp_x(sv)$ at $s = 0$ ⟦Chapter7/GeodesicFlow · hasDerivAt_exp_smul_zero⟧.
3. *$\omega_y$ agrees with segment $i$ on the closed interval $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$* ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.path_eq_segPath⟧. Only the right end needs an argument: at an interior $t_{i+1}$ the definition uses segment $i + 1$, which starts at $x_{i+1}$, where segment $i$ ends; at $t_{m+1} = 1$ the definition uses the last segment.
4. $\omega_y$ is smooth on every closed piece free of break points, which lies in one segment ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.contDiffOn_path⟧; it takes values in $N$ on $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.path_mem⟧; it is continuous on $[0, 1]$ because it is continuous on each piece ⟦Chapter8/Piecewise · continuousOn_of_piecewise⟧; and $\omega_y(0) = x_0 = p$, $\omega_y(1) = x_{m+1} = q$. So $\omega_y \in \Omega$, with the interior break points as break set ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.path_mem_pathSpace⟧ ⟦Chapter7/Path · IsPiecewiseSmooth⟧. It is a broken geodesic ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.isBroken_path⟧, and its break points are $y$, since $\omega_y(t_j)$ is the start $x_j = y_j$ of segment $j$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.nodeVal_path⟧.
5. *Its speed on $(t_i, t_{i+1})$ is $\|v_i\|/\ell_i$* ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.norm_deriv_path⟧, because a geodesic has constant speed ⟦Chapter7/Geodesic · IsGeodesicOn.norm_deriv_eq⟧ and its velocity at $t_i$ is $v_i/\ell_i$.
6. **Formula $(\ast)$** (Milnor p. 90): $E(\omega_y) = E'(y)$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.energy_path⟧. The integral over $[0, 1]$ splits over the segments ⟦Chapter10/Broken · sum_integral_fin⟧ (additivity over a subdivision indexed by $\{0, \dots, m+1\}$, by induction from the additivity over two adjacent intervals ⟦Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/IntervalIntegral/Basic · intervalIntegral.integral_add_adjacent_intervals⟧). On a segment the speed is the constant $\|v_i\|/\ell_i$, so the energy there is $(\|v_i\|/\ell_i)^2 \ell_i$ ⟦Chapter8/Energy · energy_eq_of_norm_deriv⟧, which is $\|v_i\|^2/\ell_i = \rho_i^2/\ell_i$.

**From a path to its break points: Step 3** (Milnor p. 89). *Let $\gamma \in B$ and let $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$ be a segment. There is $w \in T_{\gamma(t_i)}N$ with $\|w\| < \delta$ such that $\gamma(\tau) = \exp_{\gamma(t_i)}\big(\frac{\tau - t_i}{\ell_i} w\big)$ for $\tau \in [t_i, t_{i+1}]$, $E_{t_i}^{t_{i+1}}(\gamma) = \|w\|^2/\ell_i$, and $\rho(\gamma(t_i), \gamma(t_{i+1})) \le \|w\|$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenPaths · BrokenSetup.exists_segVec⟧.

Write $a = t_i$, $b = t_{i+1}$, $h = \ell_i$. The path is smooth on $[a, b]$ (above), so $\tilde\gamma(s) = \gamma(hs + a)$ is a smooth curve in $N$ on $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter9/JacobiSystem · IsSmoothCurveIn⟧, and a geodesic on $(0, 1)$ by the affine change of parameter. Part VII proves that a geodesic segment is the radial geodesic of its initial velocity ⟦Chapter9/GeodesicVariation · exists_initial_ray⟧: $\tilde\gamma(s) = \exp_{\gamma(a)}(s w)$ on $[0, 1]$, where $w$ is the one-sided velocity of $\tilde\gamma$ at $0$ ⟦Chapter9/JacobiSystem · velAlong⟧, a tangent vector ⟦Chapter9/JacobiSystem · IsSmoothCurveIn.velAlong_mem⟧. The ray has the constant speed $\|w\|$ of its velocity at $0$, while $\tilde\gamma'(s) = h\,\gamma'(hs + a)$; so $\|\gamma'\| = \|w\|/h$ on $(a, b)$. Hence $E_a^b(\gamma) = (\|w\|/h)^2 h = \|w\|^2/h$ and $L_a^b(\gamma) = (\|w\|/h)\,h = \|w\|$ ⟦Chapter7/Path · pathLength_eq_of_norm_deriv⟧, and $\rho(\gamma(a), \gamma(b)) \le L_a^b(\gamma) = \|w\|$. Finally $\|w\|^2 = h\,E_a^b(\gamma) \le h\,E(\gamma) < h\,c < \delta^2$, which is Milnor's estimate $(L_{t_{i-1}}^{t_i})^2 = (t_i - t_{i-1})E_{t_{i-1}}^{t_i} \le (t_i - t_{i-1})\,c < \varepsilon^2$.

*The break points of $\gamma \in B$ lie in $U$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenPaths · BrokenSetup.nodeVal_mem_nodes⟧. They lie in $N$ because $\gamma$ does on $[0, 1]$; the distances $\rho(\gamma(t_i), \gamma(t_{i+1})) \le \|w_i\|$ are finite; and, splitting $E$ over the segments,
$$
E'(\gamma(t_1), \dots, \gamma(t_m)) \le \sum_i \frac{\|w_i\|^2}{\ell_i} = \sum_i E_{t_i}^{t_{i+1}}(\gamma) = E(\gamma) < c .
$$

*$\gamma \in B$ is the broken geodesic of its break points* (on $[0, 1]$) ⟦Chapter10/BrokenPaths · BrokenSetup.path_nodeVal⟧. For $\tau$ in segment $i$, $\omega_y(\tau) = \exp_{\gamma(t_i)}\big(\frac{\tau - t_i}{\ell_i}\, v(\gamma(t_i), \gamma(t_{i+1}))\big)$ with $y$ the break points of $\gamma$. Both ends lie in $S$ by Step 1, and $\rho(\gamma(t_i), \gamma(t_{i+1})) \le \|w\| < \delta$; the vector $w$ of Step 3 is tangent, of norm $< \delta$, and $\exp_{\gamma(t_i)} w = \gamma(t_{i+1})$. By the uniqueness clause of the join vector, $v(\gamma(t_i), \gamma(t_{i+1})) = w$, and Step 3 gives $\omega_y(\tau) = \gamma(\tau)$. This is Milnor's remark that a segment of energy this small is determined by its end points.

**Lemma 16.1 (V10.4).** *Given Milnor's data: (a) for every $y \in U$, the broken geodesic $\omega_y$ lies in $B$, its break points are $y$, and $E(\omega_y) = E'(y)$; (b) for every $\gamma \in B$, the break points of $\gamma$ lie in $U$ and $\omega_{(\gamma(t_1), \dots, \gamma(t_m))} = \gamma$ on $[0, 1]$.* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenPaths · BrokenSetup.lemma_16_1⟧

So $y \mapsto \omega_y$ and $\gamma \mapsto (\gamma(t_1), \dots, \gamma(t_m))$ are inverse bijections between $U$ and $B$, paths being compared on $[0, 1]$. Milnor's assumption $\Omega^c \ne \emptyset$ is not used, so the formal lemma is slightly stronger; the image, which Milnor calls an open subset of $M \times \dots \times M$, is identified as $U$ (open by VIII.3); and the claim that the correspondence is a homeomorphism needs the metric of $\Omega$ (VIII.10) and is proved in VIII.14.

### VIII.3 The manifold of break points and the smoothness of $E'$

Milnor gives $B$ the differentiable structure of an open subset of $M \times \dots \times M$ (p. 89). For subsets of Euclidean space nothing needs to be said; for slice submanifolds the products and their open parts must be built, and so must the notion of a function that is smooth because it is locally the restriction of an ambient smooth function.

**Products and open parts.** Given slice charts $\sigma_j$ of $N$, $j$ in a finite index set, their product $y \mapsto \big((\sigma_j(y_j))_1, (\sigma_j(y_j))_2\big)_j$ is a slice chart of $N^\iota \subseteq V^\iota$ ⟦Chapter10/Product · SliceChart.pi⟧. Its domain is the product of the domains ⟦Chapter10/Product · SliceChart.pi_source⟧; its target $\{(z, w) : (z_j, w_j) \in \text{target of } \sigma_j \text{ for all } j\}$ is open as a finite intersection of preimages of open sets; it is smooth in both directions coordinate by coordinate; and $y \in N^\iota$ exactly when every second component vanishes, which is the slice condition. Its parametrization is the product of the parametrizations ⟦Chapter10/Product · sliceParam_pi⟧ ⟦Chapter1/ManifoldSubmanifold · sliceParam⟧. Hence *$N^\iota$ is a slice submanifold of $V^\iota$ modelled on $Z^\iota$* ⟦Chapter10/Product · isSliceSubmanifold_pi⟧ ⟦Milnor/Chapter2/Submanifold · IsSliceSubmanifold⟧. A slice chart of $S$ restricted to an open set $O$, with any point of $S \cap O$ in its domain as base point, is a slice chart of $S \cap O$ ⟦Chapter10/Product · SliceChart.inter⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Product · sliceParam_inter⟧: on $O$, membership in $S \cap O$ is membership in $S$. So *an open part of a slice submanifold is a slice submanifold* ⟦Chapter10/Product · IsSliceSubmanifold.inter_isOpen⟧, and in particular $N^m \cap O$ is one for every open $O \subseteq V^m$ ⟦Chapter10/Product · isSliceSubmanifold_pi_inter⟧. The chart used below at a point $y$ is the product of the charts chosen at the $y_j$, restricted to $O$ ⟦Chapter10/Product · piInterChart⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Product · mem_piInterChart_source⟧.

**The tangent space.** *The tangent space of $N^m \cap O$ at $y$ is $T_{y_1}N \times \dots \times T_{y_m}N$* ⟦Chapter10/Product · tangentSubspace_pi_inter⟧. The tangent space of a slice submanifold, as a subspace of the ambient space ⟦Chapter1/ManifoldSubmanifold · tangentSubspace⟧, is the range of the derivative of the parametrization of any slice chart ⟦Chapter1/ManifoldSubmanifold · tangentSubspace_eq_range_fderiv_sliceParam⟧. For the product chart the parametrization is $z \mapsto (x_j(z_j))_j$, whose derivative is $z \mapsto (dx_j(z_j))_j$, component by component, and the range of that map is the product of the ranges.

**Functions smooth in Milnor's sense.** *A function $f$ on a slice submanifold $S$ that agrees, on the points of $S$ in an open set $O \ni y$, with a function $F$ smooth on $O$ is smooth at $y$ as a function on the manifold $S$* ⟦Chapter10/Product · contMDiffAt_of_locally_eq⟧: $F$ composed with the inclusion is smooth at $y$ ⟦Chapter1/ManifoldSubmanifold · contMDiffAt_comp_val⟧, and $f$ agrees with it on the neighbourhood $\{x \in S : x \in O\}$ of $y$.

**$U$ is open in $N^m$ (V10.4 (i)).** The vertices depend affinely on the break points ⟦Chapter10/BrokenEnergy · contDiff_vert⟧. The gaps $g(y) = (\rho(x_i, x_{i+1}))_{0 \le i \le m}$, a point of $[0, \infty]^{m+1}$ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenEnergy · BrokenSetup.gaps⟧, depend continuously on $y \in N^m$ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenEnergy · BrokenSetup.continuousOn_gaps⟧, because $\rho$ is continuous on $N \times N$ with values in $[0, \infty]$ ⟦Chapter7/Metric · continuousOn_riemDist⟧. *There is an open $O \subseteq V^m$ with $U = N^m \cap O$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenEnergy · BrokenSetup.exists_isOpen_nodes⟧. The set
$$
T = \Big\{r \in [0, \infty]^{m+1} : r_i < \infty \text{ for all } i,\ \sum_i \frac{r_i^2}{\ell_i} < c\Big\}
$$
is open: the condition that all $r_i$ are finite defines an open set, the sum is continuous on it (the real part is continuous away from $\infty$), and $T$ is the part of that open set where a continuous function is $< c$. Continuity of $g$ on $N^m$ produces an open $O$ with $g^{-1}(T) \cap N^m = O \cap N^m$, and $g^{-1}(T) \cap N^m$ is $U$ by definition. So $U$ is a slice submanifold of $V^m$ of dimension $m \dim Z$, with tangent space $\prod_j T_{y_j}N$: the manifold whose differentiable structure Milnor takes over from the product.

**$E'$ is smooth (Theorem 16.2, first assertion; V10.6 (i)).** *Every $y_0 \in U$ has an open neighbourhood $O$ in $V^m$ with a function $F$, smooth on $O$, such that $E' = F$ on $U \cap O$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenEnergy · BrokenSetup.exists_smooth_energy'⟧. For each $i$, the consecutive vertices $x_i(y_0), x_{i+1}(y_0)$ lie in $S$ at distance $< \delta$ (VIII.2), so the data provide an open $O_{2,i} \ni (x_i(y_0), x_{i+1}(y_0))$ and a map $G_i$ smooth on it, as in Corollary 10.8. With the affine maps $\varphi_i(y) = (x_i(y), x_{i+1}(y))$, put
$$
O = \bigcap_i \varphi_i^{-1}(O_{2,i}), \qquad F(y) = \sum_i \frac{\|G_i(\varphi_i(y))\|^2}{\ell_i},
$$
smooth on $O$ because the squared norm is smooth. For $y \in U \cap O$, $G_i(\varphi_i(y))$ is a tangent vector at $x_i$ of norm $< \delta$ with $\exp_{x_i} G_i(\varphi_i(y)) = x_{i+1}$, and $\rho(x_i, x_{i+1}) < \delta$; by the uniqueness clause of the join vector it is $v(x_i, x_{i+1})$, whose norm is $\rho(x_i, x_{i+1})$. So $E'(y) = F(y)$. Milnor says that $E'$ depends smoothly on the break points because of the explicit formula. The formula alone does not show it, since $\rho$ is not smooth on the diagonal: what is smooth is $\rho^2 = \|v\|^2$, with $v$ smooth by Corollary 10.8.

Two consequences: *$E'$ is continuous on $U$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenEnergy · BrokenSetup.continuousOn_energy'⟧, since near each point it agrees on $U$ with a continuous function; and *for every open $O \subseteq V^m$ with $N^m \cap O \subseteq U$, $E'$ is a smooth function on the manifold $N^m \cap O$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenEnergy · BrokenSetup.contMDiff_energy'⟧, by the criterion for functions smooth in Milnor's sense at each point. The manifold $U$ itself is one such $N^m \cap O$.

### VIII.4 $B^a$ is compact, and a possible misprint on p. 91

**Theorem 16.2, compactness (V10.6 (ii)).** *For $a < c$ the set $\{y \in U : E'(y) \le a\}$ is compact.* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenEnergy · BrokenSetup.isCompact_sublevel⟧

This is compactness for the set of break points; the set $B^a \subseteq \Omega$ is identified with it in VIII.14.

*Proof as formalized.* Let $K = S^m$, compact as a product of compact sets ⟦Mathlib/Topology/Compactness/Compact · isCompact_univ_pi⟧. The set lies in $K$, because the vertices of a point of $U$ lie in $S$ (VIII.2). Two cases.

1. *$\rho(p, q) = \infty$.* Then the set is empty: for $y \in U$, $\rho(p, x_m) \le \sqrt c$ because $x_m \in S$, and $\rho(x_m, q) < \infty$ by the definition of $U$, so $\rho(p, q) < \infty$ by the triangle inequality, a contradiction.
2. *$\rho(p, q) < \infty$.* Then every gap is finite on all of $K$: $\rho(x_i, x_{i+1}) \le \rho(x_i, p) + \rho(p, x_{i+1})$, where $x_i \in S$ for $i \le m$, and $x_{i+1}$ lies in $S$ or is $q$. So $E'$ is continuous on $K$, as a composite of the continuous gaps and the real part on finite values; and the set equals $K \cap E'^{-1}(-\infty, a]$, because a point of $K$ with $E' \le a < c$ has finite gaps and hence lies in $U$. A closed subset of the compact $K$ is compact.

**What Milnor leaves implicit, and a misprint.** Milnor identifies $B^a$ with a set of tuples closed in the compact $S \times \dots \times S$ (p. 91). The set is closed only because the distances there are finite; with $[0, \infty]$-valued distances a tuple with an infinite gap would otherwise pass the test. Only the last gap can be infinite, and only if $\rho(p, q) = \infty$, which never happens for connected $M$; the formal proof treats that case separately. As reproduced in the scan, the condition defining the tuples carries the exponent $c$ instead of $2$ and has no visible summation sign. The exponent looks like a misprint in the printed text; the missing $\Sigma$ is probably a defect of the scan, which drops symbols from displays (Findings, §§16–19, marked *(scan?)*). The correct condition is the energy $(\ast)$, $\sum_i \rho(p_{i-1}, p_i)^2/(t_i - t_{i-1}) \le a$, and that is the formal one: $E'(p_1, \dots, p_{k-1}) \le a$ ⟦Chapter10/Broken · BrokenSetup.energy'⟧.

### VIII.5 The critical points of $E'$

Milnor (p. 91) observes that every geodesic is a broken geodesic, and that by the first variation formula the critical points of $E'$ are clearly the unbroken geodesics. The formal proof needs four things the sentence takes for granted: every tangent vector of the manifold of break points is the velocity of a curve; a smooth curve of break points yields a variation through broken geodesics, smooth on each strip; the first variation at a broken geodesic reduces to its velocity jumps; and criticality must force every jump to vanish, which the proof obtains by testing the derivative against the vector of all the jumps.

**Curves.** *Every tangent vector $w$ of a slice submanifold $S$ at $y_0$ is the velocity at $0$ of a smooth curve in $S$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenVariation · exists_curve_of_mem_tangentSubspace⟧. With $x$ the parametrization of the slice chart chosen at $y_0$ and $z_0$ the chart value of $y_0$, write $w = dx_{z_0}(z)$ (the tangent space is the range of $dx_{z_0}$). The curve $u \mapsto x(z_0 + uz)$ is defined on the open set of $u$ for which $z_0 + uz$ lies in the chart's target ⟦Chapter1/ManifoldSubmanifold · isOpen_toChart_target⟧, is smooth there ⟦Chapter1/ManifoldSubmanifold · contDiffOn_sliceParam⟧, takes values in $S$ ⟦Chapter1/ManifoldSubmanifold · sliceParam_mem⟧, passes through $y_0$ at $u = 0$ ⟦Chapter1/ManifoldSubmanifold · sliceParam_toChart⟧, and has velocity $dx_{z_0}(z) = w$ there by the chain rule ⟦Chapter1/ManifoldSubmanifold · contDiffAt_sliceParam⟧.

**Variations through broken geodesics.** *Let $u \mapsto y(u)$ be smooth on an open set $U_0 \ni 0$ with values in $U$. Then there is $\varepsilon > 0$ with $(-\varepsilon, \varepsilon) \subseteq U_0$ such that for every segment $i$ the map $(u, s) \mapsto \omega_{y(u)}(t_i + s\,\ell_i)$ is smooth on $(-\varepsilon, \varepsilon) \times [0, 1]$ and a geodesic in $s$ for each $u$, and $u \mapsto \omega_{y(u)}$ is a variation with break points $t_1, \dots, t_m$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenVariation · BrokenSetup.exists_variation_path⟧ ⟦Chapter8/Energy · IsVariation⟧.

*Proof as formalized.* Fix a segment $i$, and let $c_0(u)$, $c_1(u)$ be the $i$-th and $(i+1)$-th vertices of $y(u)$, smooth in $u$.

1. Corollary 10.8 at $(c_0(0), c_1(0))$ gives an open $O_2$ and a map $G$ smooth on it. On $U_1 = U_0 \cap (c_0, c_1)^{-1}(O_2)$, open and containing $0$, the join vector $v_i(u) = v(c_0(u), c_1(u))$ equals $G(c_0(u), c_1(u))$ by uniqueness, so $u \mapsto v_i(u)$ is smooth on $U_1$, with tangent values.
2. The base ray $s \mapsto \exp_{c_0(0)}(s\,v_i(0))$ is a geodesic on some $(-\delta', 1 + \delta')$. Part VII's theorem on the smooth dependence of geodesics on their initial data over a compact interval ⟦Chapter9/GeodesicVariation · raysGood_of_isGeodesicOn⟧ ⟦Chapter9/GeodesicVariation · RaysGood⟧ provides $\varepsilon_i, \eta_i > 0$ such that $(u, s) \mapsto \exp_{c_0(u)}(s\,v_i(u))$ is smooth on $(-\varepsilon_i, \varepsilon_i) \times (-\eta_i, 1 + \eta_i)$ and a geodesic in $s$ for each $u$.
3. Let $\varepsilon$ be the least of the $\varepsilon_i$. On the $i$-th strip, $\omega_{y(u)}(t_i + s\ell_i)$ is that ray at time $s$ (VIII.2, item 3), so the strip map is smooth on $(-\varepsilon, \varepsilon) \times [0, 1]$ and a geodesic in $s$.
4. The axioms of a variation: the paths take values in $N$; their ends are $p$ and $q$ for every $u$, since each $\omega_{y(u)}$ lies in $\Omega$; and on every strip $(-\varepsilon, \varepsilon) \times [a, b]$ with no break point inside $(a, b)$ the map is smooth, because $[a, b]$ lies in one segment and the map is the strip map composed with $(u, \tau) \mapsto (u, (\tau - t_i)/\ell_i)$.

Step 2 is the content that Milnor's sentence hides: a geodesic segment depends smoothly on its end points over the whole segment, not only near its initial point, and this is a compactness argument on $[0, 1]$.

*The variation field at the break points is the velocity of the curve of break points* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenVariation · BrokenSetup.varField_path_node⟧: if $y'(0)$ is the velocity of $y$ at $0$, the variation field ⟦Chapter8/Energy · varField⟧ at $t_j$ is $y'(0)_j$, because $\omega_{y(u)}(t_j) = y(u)_j$ for all $u$ near $0$ (VIII.2, item 4).

**The first variation at a broken geodesic.** The first variation of Part VII at a path $\omega$ in the direction of a field $X$ is ⟦Chapter8/FirstVariation · firstVariation⟧
$$
\mathrm{FV}_\omega(X) = -\sum_{t \in (0,1)} \langle X(t), \Delta_t\omega'\rangle - \int_0^1 \langle X(t), A(t)\rangle\,dt,
$$
with $\Delta_t$ the jump of the velocity ⟦Chapter8/Energy · jump⟧ and $A$ the acceleration ⟦Chapter8/FirstVariation · accel⟧. *For $y \in U$ and every $X$, $\mathrm{FV}_{\omega_y}(X) = -\sum_j \langle X(t_j), \Delta_{t_j}\omega_y'\rangle$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenVariation · BrokenSetup.firstVariation_path⟧. A point $\tau \in (0, 1)$ that is not an interior break point lies in an open segment $(t_i, t_{i+1})$ (if $\tau = t_i$ with $i > 0$, or $\tau = t_{i+1}$ with $i < m$, it would be a break point); there $\omega_y$ is a geodesic, so its acceleration, the tangential part of $\omega_y''$, vanishes ⟦Chapter7/Geodesic · IsGeodesicOn.tanProj_deriv_deriv⟧. The integrand of the integral term therefore vanishes off the finite, hence null, set of break points and $1$, and the integral is $0$. The sum over the jumps reduces to the finite sum over the break points, $\omega_y$ being differentiable elsewhere in $(0, 1)$ ⟦Chapter8/FirstVariation · finsum_jump_eq_sum⟧; all break points lie in $(0, 1)$, and the sum is reindexed by $j$ since $t$ is injective. *The jumps are tangent at the break points* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenVariation · BrokenSetup.prj_jump_path⟧, as differences of one-sided velocities of a path in $N$ ⟦Chapter8/Critical · prj_jump⟧.

**The derivative of $E'$.** *Let $O \subseteq V^m$ be open with $N^m \cap O \subseteq U$, and $F$ smooth on $V^m$ with $F = E'$ on $N^m \cap O$. For $y_0 \in N^m \cap O$ and every $w \in T_{y_0}(N^m \cap O) = \prod_j T_{y_{0,j}}N$,*
$$
dF_{y_0}(w) = -2 \sum_j \langle w_j, \Delta_{t_j}\omega_{y_0}'\rangle .
$$
⟦Chapter10/BrokenVariation · BrokenSetup.fderiv_eq_jumps⟧. Take a curve $y(u)$ in $N^m \cap O$ with velocity $w$ at $0$, and the variation $\alpha(u) = \omega_{y(u)}$. The first variation formula, Theorem 12.2 of Part VII ⟦Chapter8/FirstVariation · first_variation⟧, says that $u \mapsto E(\alpha(u))$ has derivative $2\,\mathrm{FV}_{\omega_{y_0}}(W)$ at $0$, with $W$ the variation field. By the chain rule $u \mapsto F(y(u))$ has derivative $dF_{y_0}(w)$ at $0$. The two functions agree for $|u| < \varepsilon$, because $F(y(u)) = E'(y(u)) = E(\omega_{y(u)})$ by formula $(\ast)$. Derivatives are unique, so $dF_{y_0}(w) = 2\,\mathrm{FV}_{\omega_{y_0}}(W)$, and the first variation at a broken geodesic, with $W(t_j) = w_j$, gives the formula.

**Theorem 16.2, critical points (V10.9).** *Let $O$ and $F$ be as above and $y_0 \in N^m \cap O$. Then $y_0$ is a critical point ⟦Chapter1/Manifold · IsCriticalPointM⟧ of $E' = F|(N^m \cap O)$ if and only if the broken geodesic $\omega_{y_0}$ is an unbroken geodesic ⟦Chapter8/Critical · IsUnbrokenGeodesic⟧, that is, by Corollary 12.3, a critical point of $E$ on $\Omega$.* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenVariation · BrokenSetup.isCriticalPointM_iff⟧

*Proof as formalized.* A point of a submanifold is critical for the restriction of an ambient function exactly when the ambient derivative vanishes on the tangent space ⟦Chapter1/ManifoldSubmanifold · isCriticalPointM_comp_val_iff_forall_tangent⟧.

- ($\Rightarrow$) Test with the jumps themselves: $\Delta = (\Delta_{t_j}\omega_{y_0}')_j$ is tangent to $N^m \cap O$ at $y_0$, since each jump is tangent at $y_{0,j}$ and the tangent space is the product. Then $0 = dF_{y_0}(\Delta) = -2\sum_j \|\Delta_j\|^2$, so every jump vanishes. By Corollary 12.3 ⟦Chapter8/Critical · corollary_12_3⟧, an unbroken geodesic is the same as a critical path, and a path is critical exactly when its first variation vanishes on every $X \in T\Omega$ ⟦Chapter8/Critical · isCriticalPath_iff⟧ ⟦Chapter8/Energy · pathTangent⟧; with all jumps zero, the first variation at the broken geodesic is $0$ for every $X$.
- ($\Leftarrow$) An unbroken geodesic is differentiable at every $t_j \in (0, 1)$, so its jumps vanish ⟦Chapter8/Energy · jump_eq_zero⟧ and $dF_{y_0}$ vanishes on the tangent space.

The formal statement reads $E'$ through an ambient smooth extension $F$, as Part I reads functions on submanifolds, and holds for every such $F$ and $O$; such an $F$ exists near any compact sublevel set (VIII.8).

**Every geodesic of energy $< c$ is a broken geodesic** (Milnor p. 91; V10.9 (iv)). An unbroken geodesic is a broken geodesic for every subdivision ⟦Chapter10/Finite · BrokenSetup.isBroken_of_isUnbrokenGeodesic⟧, so a geodesic $\gamma \in \Omega$ with $E(\gamma) < c$ lies in $B$, and Lemma 16.1 applies: *its break points $y$ lie in $U$, $\omega_y = \gamma$ on $[0, 1]$, $\omega_y$ is an unbroken geodesic, and $E'(y) = E(\gamma)$* ⟦Chapter10/Finite · BrokenSetup.eqOn_path_nodeVal_of_isUnbrokenGeodesic⟧. Smoothness on $[0, 1]$ and the geodesic property pass along agreement on $[0, 1]$, and the energy only sees $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter8/Hessian · energy_congr⟧. So the critical points of $E'$ correspond to the geodesics from $p$ to $q$ of energy $< c$, that is, of length $< \sqrt c$, an unbroken geodesic having constant speed and so $E = L^2$.

### VIII.6 Broken Jacobi fields for the subdivision of §16, and Lemma 15.4 for it

Milnor finishes Theorem 16.2 in two sentences (p. 92): the tangent space of $B$ at a geodesic $\gamma$ is identified with the space $T\Omega_\gamma(t_0, \dots, t_k)$ of broken Jacobi fields of §15, and the equality of the indices and nullities is then an immediate consequence of Lemma 15.4. But Lemma 15.4, as proved in §15 and in Part VII ⟦Chapter9/IndexReduction · lemma_15_4⟧, concerns a subdivision fine in the sense of §15 ⟦Chapter9/BrokenJacobi · IsFine⟧: every piece has length at most $h_0 = 1/(K + 1)$, with $K$ a bound along $\gamma$ for $\|P'\|^2 + \|R(V, P\,\cdot\,)V\|$, which makes $E_{**}$ positive definite on the fields vanishing at the division points. The subdivision of §16 was chosen for a different purpose and need not be that fine. This is a hidden hypothesis. The formal proof isolates the two facts that Lemma 15.4 uses: (1) broken Jacobi fields with arbitrary values at the break points exist and are unique; (2) $E_{**}$ is positive definite on the fields vanishing at the break points. For §16, (1) holds because each segment is a radial geodesic shorter than a normal radius, and (2) because a broken geodesic minimizes the energy among the paths through its break points, with (1) excluding null vectors.

The objects are those of Part VII (§0.9): Jacobi fields along $\gamma$, smooth and tangent on $[0, 1]$ and solving the Jacobi equation ⟦Chapter8/Jacobi · IsJacobiField⟧ ⟦Chapter8/Jacobi · jacobiExpr⟧; conjugate parameters $a \ne b$, at which a nonzero Jacobi field vanishes ⟦Chapter9/JacobiSpace · IsConjugate⟧; broken Jacobi fields, which agree on every piece with a Jacobi field ⟦Chapter9/BrokenJacobi · IsBrokenJacobi⟧; the space $T\Omega_\gamma(t_0, \dots, t_k)$ of fields of $T\Omega_\gamma$ that vanish outside $[0, \tau]$ and are broken Jacobi fields there ⟦Chapter9/BrokenJacobi · brokenSpace⟧, here with $\tau = 1$ and the interior break points as division points; Milnor's $T'$, the fields vanishing at the division points and outside $[0, \tau]$ ⟦Chapter9/BrokenJacobi · nodeZero⟧; the fields vanishing outside $[0, \tau]$ ⟦Chapter9/BrokenJacobi · fieldsUpTo⟧; and the index form $E_{**}$ ⟦Chapter8/Hessian · energyHessian⟧.

Throughout this section $y \in U$ and $\gamma = \omega_y$ is an unbroken geodesic, hence a smooth curve in $N$ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.isSmoothCurveIn_path⟧ ⟦Chapter9/JacobiSystem · IsUnbrokenGeodesic.isSmoothCurveIn⟧.

**Jacobi fields under an affine change of parameter.** Milnor's Lemma 14.3 is stated for variations parametrized by $[0, 1]$, so every segment must be rescaled, and Jacobi fields must follow. The derivative of $s \mapsto f(hs + a)$ is $h f'(hs + a)$ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · deriv_comp_affine⟧, and the curvature term satisfies $R(h\,u_1, u_2)(h\,u_3) = h^2 R(u_1, u_2)u_3$ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · curvR_smul_smul⟧ ⟦Chapter8/SecondVariation · curvR⟧. Hence *if $J$ is a Jacobi field along $\gamma$ and $0 \le a$, $0 < h$, $a + h \le 1$, then $s \mapsto J(hs + a)$ is a Jacobi field along $s \mapsto \gamma(hs + a)$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · IsJacobiField.comp_affine⟧: the second covariant derivative and the curvature term both scale by $h^2$. On $[0, 1]$ the rescaled $i$-th segment of $\gamma$ is the radial geodesic of the join vector, $\gamma(\ell_i s + t_i) = \exp_{x_i}(s\,v_i)$ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.path_affine_eq⟧.

**No conjugate points at the ends of a segment.** *For every $i$, $t_i$ and $t_{i+1}$ are not conjugate along $\gamma$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.not_isConjugate_seg⟧. Suppose $J$ is a Jacobi field along $\gamma$ with $J(t_i) = J(t_{i+1}) = 0$ and $J(t) \ne 0$ somewhere. Its rescaling $\tilde J(s) = J(\ell_i s + t_i)$ is a Jacobi field along the rescaled segment, hence along the ray $s \mapsto \exp_{x_i}(s v_i)$, since being a Jacobi field depends only on the curve on $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter9/JacobiVariation · IsJacobiField.congr_curve⟧. Milnor's data give a normal radius $\varepsilon \ge \delta > \|v_i\|$ at $x_i$. Remark 14.5 for a normal radius ⟦Chapter9/JacobiVariation · remark_14_5_of_isNormalRadius⟧ says that a Jacobi field along $s \mapsto \exp_q(s v)$, $\|v\| < \varepsilon$, vanishing at both ends vanishes on $[0, 1]$. So $J = 0$ on $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$. A Jacobi field that vanishes on a non-degenerate interval vanishes identically ⟦Chapter9/IndexReduction · jacobiSpace_eq_zero_of_eqOn⟧ (uniqueness for the linear Jacobi equation), applied to $J$ read on $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter9/JacobiSpace · IsJacobiField.comp_projIcc_mem⟧; this contradicts $J(t) \ne 0$. Here, and in the companion lemma of VIII.7, the normal radius in Milnor's data is used.

**Uniqueness.** *A broken Jacobi field $X$ (division points $t_1, \dots, t_m$, $\tau = 1$) that vanishes at every $t_j$ is zero* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.brokenSpace_eq_zero⟧. For $t \in [0, 1]$ in segment $i$, no break point lies in $(t_i, t_{i+1})$ (index arithmetic), so $X$ agrees on $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$ with a Jacobi field $J$. $X$ vanishes at every division point: at $t_0 = 0$ and $t_{m+1} = 1$ because it lies in $T\Omega_\gamma$, at the $t_j$ by hypothesis. So $J(t_i) = J(t_{i+1}) = 0$, $J = 0$ on $[0, 1]$ by the previous step, and $X(t) = J(t) = 0$. Outside $[0, 1]$, $X$ vanishes by definition.

**Existence.** *For tangent vectors $\xi_j \in T_{y_j}N$ there is a broken Jacobi field $X$ with $X(t_j) = \xi_j$ for all $j$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.exists_brokenSpace_node⟧.

1. Prescribe values at all division points, $0$ at $t_0$ and $t_{m+1}$ and $\xi_j$ at $t_j$; they are tangent at the corresponding points of $\gamma$ ⟦Chapter9/JacobiSystem · mem_tanAt⟧ ⟦Chapter9/JacobiSystem · tanAt⟧.
2. On each segment the ends are not conjugate, so $J \mapsto (J(t_i), J(t_{i+1}))$ is a bijection from the Jacobi fields along $\gamma$ ⟦Chapter9/JacobiSpace · jacobiSpace⟧ onto $T_{\gamma(t_i)}N \times T_{\gamma(t_{i+1})}N$ ⟦Chapter9/GeodesicVariation · jacEvalEnds_bijective_of_not_isConjugate⟧. Choose $J_i$ with the prescribed values at $t_i$ and $t_{i+1}$.
3. Glue: $X(t) = J_{i(t)}(t)$ on $[0, 1]$, $i(t)$ the segment of $t$, and $X = 0$ outside. On each closed segment $X = J_i$; at an interior $t_{i+1}$ the formula uses $J_{i+1}$, but $J_{i+1}(t_{i+1}) = J_i(t_{i+1})$, both being the prescribed value. So $X$ is a broken Jacobi field with the prescribed values.
4. $X$ lies in $T\Omega_\gamma$: it is smooth on every piece free of break points and therefore continuous on $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter8/Piecewise · continuousOn_of_piecewise⟧, it is tangent because Jacobi fields are, and it vanishes at $0$ and $1$.

The values at the break points are tangent ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.brokenSpace_node_mem⟧, so $X \mapsto (X(t_1), \dots, X(t_m))$ is a linear map into $\prod_j T_{y_j}N$ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.brokenNodeMap⟧, injective by uniqueness and surjective by existence ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.brokenNodeMap_bijective⟧. This gives the linear isomorphism
$$
T\Omega_\gamma(t_0, \dots, t_k) \cong T_{y_1}N \times \dots \times T_{y_m}N
$$
⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.brokenEquiv⟧ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.brokenEquiv_apply⟧, the first half of Milnor's identification of $TB_\gamma$ with the broken Jacobi fields. The second half, that the variation fields of curves in $B$ are these fields, is VIII.7.

**$E_{**}$ on broken Jacobi fields.** *For a broken Jacobi field $X$ and any $Y \in T\Omega_\gamma$,*
$$
E_{**}(X, Y) = -2\sum_j \big\langle Y(t_j),\ P\,\Delta_{t_j} X'\big\rangle
$$
⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.energyHessian_brokenSpace_eq⟧, with $P$ the tangent projection at $\gamma(t_j)$. This is Part VII's second variation formula for fields that agree near every point of $(0, 1)$ off a finite set with a Jacobi field ⟦Chapter9/IndexForm · energyHessian_eq_jumps⟧, the local Jacobi fields being supplied by the broken Jacobi structure ⟦Chapter9/BrokenJacobi · IsBrokenJacobi.eventuallyEq⟧; the sum is then restricted to the break points, all in $(0, 1)$, and reindexed. In particular *broken Jacobi fields are perpendicular to the fields vanishing at the break points* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.energyHessian_brokenSpace_nodeZero⟧, Lemma 15.3 for §16.

**Positivity, first the semi-definite form.** *The broken geodesic minimizes the energy among paths through its break points*: for every $\omega \in \Omega$,
$$
E'(\omega(t_1), \dots, \omega(t_m)) \le E(\omega)
$$
⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.energy'_nodeVal_le⟧. Split $E$ over the segments; on each, $\rho(\omega(t_i), \omega(t_{i+1}))^2 \le (L_{t_i}^{t_{i+1}})^2 \le \ell_i E_{t_i}^{t_{i+1}}(\omega)$ by Schwarz's inequality. *Consequently $E_{**}(Y, Y) \ge 0$ for every $Y \in T\Omega_\gamma$ vanishing at the $t_j$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.energyHessian_nodeZero_nonneg⟧.

1. Lemma 11.A for one field ⟦Chapter8/FirstVariation · exists_variation_one⟧ gives the variation $\alpha(u)(t) = \exp_{\gamma(t)}(u\,Y(t))$, $|u| < \varepsilon$, with variation field $Y$ on $[0, 1]$.
2. It keeps the break points fixed: $\alpha(u)(t_j) = \exp_{y_j}(0) = y_j$. So $f(u) = E(\alpha(u)) \ge E'(y) = E(\gamma) = f(0)$: $f$ has a local minimum at $0$.
3. Remark 13.5 ⟦Chapter8/Hessian · hasDerivAt_deriv_energy⟧ says that $f''(0) = E_{**}(W, W)$ for the variation field $W$, and $W = Y$ on $[0, 1]$, which is all $E_{**}$ sees ⟦Chapter8/Hessian · secondVariation_congr⟧ ⟦Chapter8/Hessian · energyHessian_apply⟧.
4. *A local minimum has nonnegative second derivative* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · nonneg_of_isLocalMin_of_hasDerivAt⟧, the step of Milnor's Lemma 13.6. If $f''(0) < 0$, then, since $f'(0) = 0$ at a minimum, the second derivative test ⟦Mathlib/Analysis/Calculus/DerivativeTest · isLocalMax_of_deriv_deriv_neg⟧ makes $0$ a local maximum as well; $f$ is then constant near $0$, and $f''(0) = 0$, a contradiction.

Milnor's own argument in §15 is the minimality of each short segment; here it is the minimality of the broken geodesic among the paths with the same break points, which needs no fineness.

**Positivity, then the definite form.** *If moreover $Y$ vanishes outside $[0, 1]$ and $Y \ne 0$, then $E_{**}(Y, Y) > 0$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.energyHessian_nodeZero_pos⟧. Suppose $E_{**}(Y, Y) = 0$. Then $Y$ is a null vector of $E_{**}$. Let $X$ be arbitrary, and $X_b$ the broken Jacobi field with $X_b(t_j) = X(t_j)$ (existence); then $X - X_b$ vanishes at the $t_j$.

- $E_{**}(Y, X_b) = 0$ by perpendicularity and the symmetry of $E_{**}$ ⟦Chapter8/Hessian · energyHessian_isSymm⟧.
- $E_{**}(Y, Z) = 0$ for $Z = X - X_b$, by Cauchy–Schwarz for a semi-definite form. With $b = E_{**}(Y, Z)$ and $a = E_{**}(Z, Z) \ge 0$, every $Y + rZ$ vanishes at the $t_j$, so $0 \le E_{**}(Y + rZ, Y + rZ) = 2rb + r^2a$ for all $r$. If $b \ne 0$, the choice $r = -b/(a+1)$ gives $2rb + r^2 a = -b^2(a + 2)/(a+1)^2 < 0$.

So $E_{**}(Y, X) = 0$ for all $X$, and by Theorem 14.1 ⟦Chapter8/Jacobi · theorem_14_1⟧ the null vector $Y$ is a Jacobi field. It vanishes at $t_0 = 0$ and at $t_1$, which is $1$ if $m = 0$ and a break point otherwise; since $t_0$ and $t_1$ are not conjugate, $Y = 0$ on $[0, 1]$, and outside by hypothesis. That contradicts $Y \ne 0$.

**The splitting.** *Inside the fields vanishing outside $[0, 1]$, the broken Jacobi fields and the fields vanishing at the break points are complementary subspaces* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.isCompl_brokenSpace_nodeZero⟧, Milnor's $T\Omega_\gamma = T\Omega_\gamma(t_0, \dots, t_k) \oplus T'$. They meet in $0$ by uniqueness, and $X = X_b + (X - X_b)$, with $X_b$ the broken Jacobi field matching $X$ at the $t_j$.

**Lemma 15.4 for the subdivision of §16, the index.** *The index of $E_{**}$ at $\gamma$ equals the index of its restriction to the broken Jacobi fields* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.formIndex_brokenSpace⟧. The index of a bilinear form is the supremum, in $\{0, 1, \dots, \infty\}$, of the dimensions of the finite-dimensional subspaces on which it is negative definite ⟦Chapter9/FormIndex · formIndex⟧. The index of $E_{**}$ equals its index on the fields vanishing outside $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter9/IndexReduction · indexUpTo_one⟧ ⟦Chapter9/IndexReduction · indexUpTo⟧. On that space Part VII's abstract Lemma 15.B applies ⟦Chapter9/FormIndex · formIndex_eq_restrict_of_isCompl⟧: if the space is $A \oplus C$, with $A$ and $C$ perpendicular in both orders and the form positive definite on $C$, then the form and its restriction to $A$ have the same index. Here $A$ is the broken Jacobi fields and $C$ the fields vanishing at the break points, perpendicularity is the previous step, and positivity on $C$ is the definite form above, applicable because the elements of $C$ vanish outside $[0, 1]$. The index is finally transported along the identification of $A$ with the broken Jacobi fields themselves ⟦Chapter9/FormIndex · formIndex_eq_of_equiv⟧.

**Lemma 15.4 for the subdivision of §16, the nullity.** *The null space of $E_{**}$ restricted to the broken Jacobi fields has dimension the nullity of $E_{**}$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenSplitting · BrokenSetup.finrank_ker_brokenSpace⟧ ⟦Chapter9/JacobiSpace · hessNullity⟧. By Theorem 14.1 in its nullity form ⟦Chapter9/JacobiSpace · theorem_14_1_nullity⟧ ⟦Chapter9/JacobiSpace · conjMultiplicity⟧, the nullity is the dimension of the space of Jacobi fields vanishing at $0$ and $1$ ⟦Chapter9/JacobiSpace · conjSpace⟧. The map $\varphi$ that cuts such a $J$ off to $[0, 1]$ lands in $T\Omega_\gamma$ ⟦Chapter9/IndexForm · indicator_mem_pathTangent⟧, is linear, and is injective, a Jacobi field vanishing on $[0, 1]$ being zero. Its range is exactly the null space of the restricted form:

- $\varphi J$ is a broken Jacobi field (it is $J$ on every piece), and $E_{**}(\varphi J, Y) = -2\sum_j \langle Y(t_j), P\Delta_{t_j}(\varphi J)'\rangle = 0$, since $\varphi J = J$ near each interior $t_j$ ⟦Chapter8/Energy · jump_congr⟧ and $J$ is differentiable there.
- Conversely let $X$ be a broken Jacobi field in the null space. Test it against the broken Jacobi field $Y$ with $Y(t_j) = P\Delta_{t_j}X'$, which is tangent because $P$ is idempotent ⟦Chapter8/Energy · prj_prj⟧: $0 = E_{**}(X, Y) = -2\sum_j \|P\Delta_{t_j}X'\|^2$. So every projected jump vanishes, and a broken Jacobi field without projected jumps agrees on $[0, 1]$ with a single Jacobi field $J$ ⟦Chapter9/IndexReduction · eq_jacobi_of_jumps_zero⟧, which vanishes at $0$ and $1$ because $X$ does. So $X = \varphi J$.

An injective linear map preserves dimension, which proves the claim. The file is Lemma 15.4 proved again, not applied: the fineness of §15 is replaced by the normal radius in Milnor's data and by the minimality of broken geodesics.

### VIII.7 Theorem 16.2: the index and the nullity of $E'$

Throughout, $O \subseteq V^m$ is open with $N^m \cap O \subseteq U$, $F$ is smooth on $V^m$ with $F = E'$ on $N^m \cap O$, and $y \in N^m \cap O$ is such that $\gamma = \omega_y$ is an unbroken geodesic, that is (VIII.5), a critical point of $E'$. Let $\sigma$ be the product chart at $y$ restricted to $O$, $x$ its parametrization, and $z_0$ the chart value of $y$, so that $x(z_0) = y$.

**Four lemmas.**

- *The second derivative along a line is the Hessian*: for $G$ of class $C^2$ at $z_0$, the second derivative of $u \mapsto G(z_0 + uz)$ at $0$ is $H_G(z_0)(z, z)$ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenIndex · hasDerivAt_deriv_line⟧, where the Hessian is the derivative of the derivative ⟦Chapter1/Nondegenerate · hessian⟧. Near $0$ the first derivative is $u \mapsto dG_{z_0 + uz}(z)$ (a function $C^2$ at a point is $C^2$, hence differentiable, nearby), and $dG$ has derivative $H_G(z_0)$ at $z_0$; evaluate at $z$.
- *Polarization*: two symmetric biadditive forms with the same values on the diagonal are equal ⟦Chapter10/BrokenIndex · eq_of_quadratic_eq⟧, by expanding both at $(x + y, x + y)$.
- The derivative of the parametrization of a slice chart is injective ⟦Chapter10/BrokenIndex · injective_fderiv_sliceParam'⟧: near the chart value, the chart composed with the parametrization is $z \mapsto (z, 0)$, so by the chain rule the derivative of the chart composed with $dx$ is the inclusion of $Z$ into $Z \times W$.
- *Jacobi fields along a segment are determined by their end values* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenIndex · BrokenSetup.eqOn_of_isJacobiField_seg⟧: two Jacobi fields along the ray $s \mapsto \exp_{x_i}(s v_i)$ that agree at $s = 0$ and $s = 1$ agree on $[0, 1]$. The ray is a smooth curve because $\|v_i\|$ is below a normal radius ⟦Chapter9/JacobiVariation · IsNormalRadius.isSmoothCurveIn_ray⟧, and along it $J \mapsto (J(0), J(1))$ is a bijection, Remark 14.5 in its existence form ⟦Chapter9/JacobiVariation · remark_14_5_bijective⟧; injectivity is what is used.

**The isomorphism $\Lambda$.** The derivative $dx_{z_0}$ is a linear isomorphism of $Z^m$ onto $\prod_j T_{y_j}N$ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenIndex · chartTangentEquiv⟧ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenIndex · chartTangentEquiv_apply⟧: its range is the tangent space, which is the product (VIII.3), and it is injective. Composing with the inverse of the isomorphism of VIII.6 gives
$$
\Lambda : Z^m \to T\Omega_\gamma(t_0, \dots, t_k), \qquad \Lambda z = \text{the broken Jacobi field with values } dx_{z_0}(z)_j \text{ at the } t_j
$$
⟦Chapter10/BrokenIndex · BrokenSetup.chartBrokenEquiv⟧ ⟦Chapter10/BrokenIndex · BrokenSetup.chartBrokenEquiv_node⟧.

**The second derivative of $E'$ along a line is $E_{**}$ on $\Lambda z$.** *With $H$ the Hessian of $F \circ x$ at $z_0$, $H(z, z) = E_{**}(\Lambda z, \Lambda z)$ for every $z \in Z^m$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenIndex · BrokenSetup.hessian_apply_self⟧.

1. The line $\ell(u) = z_0 + uz$ stays in the chart's target for $u$ in an open set $U_0 \ni 0$. The curve of break points $y(u) = x(\ell(u))$ is smooth, lies in $N^m \cap O \subseteq U$, has $y(0) = y$ and velocity $dx_{z_0}(z)$ at $0$.
2. By VIII.5, $\alpha(u) = \omega_{y(u)}$, $|u| < \varepsilon$, is a variation of $\gamma$ through broken geodesics, smooth on the strips.
3. Remark 13.5 ⟦Chapter8/Hessian · hasDerivAt_deriv_energy⟧: the second derivative of $u \mapsto E(\alpha(u))$ at $0$ is $E_{**}(W, W)$, with $W$ the variation field.
4. The function $G = F \circ x$ is $C^2$ at $z_0$, so by the first lemma the second derivative of $u \mapsto G(z_0 + uz)$ at $0$ is $H(z, z)$.
5. For $|u| < \varepsilon$, $G(z_0 + uz) = F(y(u)) = E'(y(u)) = E(\alpha(u))$ by formula $(\ast)$. The two functions agree near $0$, so do their derivatives, and the second derivatives at $0$ coincide: $H(z, z) = E_{**}(W, W)$.
6. It remains to show $W = \Lambda z$ on $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter8/Hessian · secondVariation_congr⟧. Fix $t$ in segment $i$.
   - At every division point the two fields agree: at $t_0$ and $t_{m+1}$ both vanish, $W$ because it is the variation field of a variation with fixed ends ⟦Chapter8/Energy · IsVariation.varField_mem⟧; at $t_j$, $W(t_j) = y'(0)_j = dx_{z_0}(z)_j = \Lambda z(t_j)$ by VIII.5 and the definition of $\Lambda$.
   - On the $i$-th strip, Lemma 14.3 of Part VII ⟦Chapter9/JacobiVariation · lemma_14_3⟧, applied to the strip variation $(u, s) \mapsto \omega_{y(u)}(t_i + s\ell_i)$ (smooth, with values in $N$, a geodesic in $s$), shows that $s \mapsto W(t_i + s\ell_i)$ is a Jacobi field along the rescaled segment, which is the ray $s \mapsto \exp_{x_i}(s v_i)$.
   - On segment $i$, $\Lambda z$ agrees with a Jacobi field $J$ along $\gamma$, and $s \mapsto J(\ell_i s + t_i)$ is a Jacobi field along the same ray (affine change of parameter).
   - The two rescaled fields agree at $s = 0$ and $s = 1$, the division-point values; by the fourth lemma they agree on $[0, 1]$, and substituting $s = (t - t_i)/\ell_i$ gives $W(t) = \Lambda z(t)$.

This is Milnor's statement that the variation field of a variation through broken geodesics is clearly a broken Jacobi field, made precise: it needs Lemma 14.3 on each segment after rescaling, and uniqueness to identify the result with $\Lambda z$.

*Hence $H(z, z') = E_{**}(\Lambda z, \Lambda z')$ for all $z, z'$* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenIndex · BrokenSetup.hessian_eq_energyHessian⟧, by polarization: both sides are symmetric, the Hessian by the symmetry of second derivatives ⟦Chapter1/Nondegenerate · hessian_symm⟧ and $E_{**}$ as before.

**Theorem 16.2, index and nullity (V10.11).** *Let $O$, $F$ and $y$ be as above, with $\omega_y$ an unbroken geodesic. Then the index of $E' = F|(N^m \cap O)$ at $y$ ⟦Chapter1/Manifold · indexM⟧ equals the index of $E_{**}$ at $\omega_y$, and the nullity of $E'$ at $y$ ⟦Chapter1/Manifold · nullityM⟧ equals the nullity of $E_{**}$ at $\omega_y$.* ⟦Chapter10/BrokenIndex · BrokenSetup.theorem_16_2_index⟧

*Proof as formalized.* The point $y$ is critical (VIII.5).

- *Index.* The index of a critical point of a function on a manifold, defined in the preferred chart, equals the index of the Hessian of $F \circ x$ in the slice chart ⟦Chapter1/ManifoldSubmanifold · index_hessian_comp_sliceParam⟧ (chart independence at a critical point, Part I). On the finite-dimensional $Z^m$, the index of the continuous form $H$ is its index in the sense of the supremum above ⟦Chapter9/FormIndex · formIndex_eq_sigNeg⟧. $\Lambda$ carries $H$ to $E_{**}$ restricted to the broken Jacobi fields, so the two indices agree ⟦Chapter9/FormIndex · formIndex_eq_of_equiv⟧, and by Lemma 15.4 for §16 the latter is the index of $E_{**}$.
- *Nullity.* The nullity of $E'$ at $y$ is the nullity of $H$ ⟦Chapter1/ManifoldSubmanifold · nullity_hessian_comp_sliceParam⟧, the dimension of its null space ⟦Chapter1/Nondegenerate · nullSpace⟧. Since $\Lambda$ is onto, $H(z, \cdot) = 0$ if and only if $E_{**}(\Lambda z, \cdot) = 0$ on the broken Jacobi fields, so the null space of $H$ is the preimage under $\Lambda$ of the null space of the restricted form; an isomorphism preserves dimension; and that dimension is the nullity of $E_{**}$ by Lemma 15.4 for §16.

What Milnor calls an immediate consequence of Lemma 15.4 thus takes Lemma 15.4 proved again (VIII.6), the identification of variation fields with broken Jacobi fields, and polarization.

### VIII.8 Theorem 16.3 in the finite-dimensional model

Milnor's proof of Theorem 16.3 is one sentence (p. 90): it follows from 16.2 and 3.5. But Theorem 3.5, as proved in Part II ⟦Chapter2/CWType · theorem_3_5'⟧, concerns a *compact* submanifold, a function that is the restriction of a smooth ambient function, and *all* critical points non-degenerate; here $U$ is not compact, $E'$ lives only on $U$, and only the critical points in $B^a$ are assumed non-degenerate. The exposition reparametrizes $E'$ on $\{E' < a'\}$, for a level $a' > a$ below the next critical value, by an increasing diffeomorphism onto $\mathbb R$. The formal proof instead extends $E'$ smoothly from a compact sublevel set to all of $V^m$, and proves Theorem 3.5 for a compact sublevel set of a function on a manifold that need not be compact.

**Smooth extension from a compact set.** *Let $K \subseteq S'$ be subsets of a finite-dimensional normed space $E$, with $K$ compact, and suppose that near every point of $K$ the function $f$ agrees on $S'$ with a function smooth on an open set. Then some smooth $F : E \to \mathbb R$ agrees with $f$ on $K$* ⟦Chapter10/SublevelCW · exists_contDiff_eq_of_locally⟧. Choose, for each $x \in K$, an open $O_x \ni x$ and $G_x$ smooth on $O_x$ with $f = G_x$ on $S' \cap O_x$; finitely many $O_{x}$ cover $K$; a smooth partition of unity $(\varphi_i)$ subordinate to this finite cover on the closed set $K$ exists ⟦Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/PartitionOfUnity · SmoothPartitionOfUnity.exists_isSubordinate⟧. Put $F = \sum_i \varphi_i G_i$, each term smooth on all of $E$ because the support of $\varphi_i$ lies in the open set where $G_i$ is smooth ⟦Chapter2/Regular · contDiff_smul_of_tsupport⟧. For $y \in K$, $\sum_i \varphi_i(y) = 1$, and $\varphi_i(y) \ne 0$ forces $y \in O_i$, where $G_i(y) = f(y)$; hence $F(y) = f(y)$.

**Passing one critical level, relative to the sublevel set below.** *Let $N \subseteq V$ be a slice submanifold, $F$ smooth on $V$, $f = F|N$, with $N^{a_0} = f^{-1}(-\infty, a_0]$ compact. Let $S$ be a finite set of non-degenerate critical points of $f$, all at level $c$, and $a' < c < a \le a_0$ such that every other critical point lies below $a'$ or above $a$. If $N^{a'}$ has the homotopy type of a finite cell complex with cell dimensions $\mathit{cells}$, then $N^a$ has that of one with cell dimensions $\mathit{cells}$ followed by the indices of the points of $S$, in some order* ⟦Chapter10/SublevelCW · IsFiniteCellComplexType.sublevel_step_of_isCompact⟧. Here *having the homotopy type of a finite cell complex* means being homotopy equivalent to a space obtained from the empty space by attaching cells of the listed dimensions one at a time along arbitrary continuous maps ⟦Chapter2/CWType · IsFiniteCellComplexType⟧ (§0.5), and the sublevel set is Milnor's $M^a$ ⟦Chapter2/Crossing · sublevel⟧ (§0.4).

1. Every slab $f^{-1}[b, b']$ with $b' \le a_0$ is compact, as a closed subset of $N^{a_0}$.
2. Let $\varepsilon_0 = \min(c - a', a - c)$. The slab $f^{-1}[c - \varepsilon_0, c + \varepsilon_0]$ is compact and all its critical points lie in $S$. Remark 3.3 for a submanifold ⟦Chapter2/SeveralCritical · remark_3_3_submanifold⟧ gives $\varepsilon_1 > 0$ and, for $\varepsilon = \min(\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_0/2)$, pairwise disjoint cells $e_p$, $p \in S$, of dimension the index of $p$, each meeting $N^{c-\varepsilon}$ exactly in its boundary sphere and lying in $N^{c+\varepsilon}$, such that $N^{c-\varepsilon} \cup \bigcup_p e_p$ is a strong deformation retract ⟦Chapter2/Crossing · IsStrongDeformationRetract⟧ of $N^{c+\varepsilon}$.
3. The slabs $[a', c - \varepsilon]$ and $[c + \varepsilon, a]$ are compact and free of critical points, so Theorem 3.1 for a submanifold ⟦Chapter2/Submanifold · theorem_3_1_submanifold⟧ gives homotopy equivalences $N^{a'} \simeq N^{c - \varepsilon}$ and $N^{c+\varepsilon} \simeq N^a$.
4. Attaching the disjoint cells one at a time to the closed set $N^{c-\varepsilon}$ of a Hausdorff space ⟦Chapter2/CWType · isFiniteCellComplexType_union_cells⟧ appends their dimensions to the cell list; the inclusion of a strong deformation retract is a homotopy equivalence ⟦Chapter2/Crossing · IsStrongDeformationRetract.exists_homotopyEquiv⟧; and the property is invariant under homotopy equivalence ⟦Chapter2/CWType · IsFiniteCellComplexType.of_homotopyEquiv⟧.

The step is relative: up to homotopy, $N^a$ is $N^{a'}$ with the cells of the level $c$ attached, whatever happens above $a$.

**Theorem 3.5 for a compact sublevel set.** *Let $N$, $F$, $f$ be as above, with $N^{a_0}$ compact, every critical point of $f$ in $N^{a_0}$ non-degenerate, and $a_0$ not a critical value. Then the critical points of $f$ below $a_0$ form a list $p_1, \dots, p_j$ without repetitions, with $f(p_1) \le \dots \le f(p_j)$, and $N^{a_0}$ has the homotopy type of the space obtained from the empty space by attaching cells of dimensions $\operatorname{index}(p_1), \dots, \operatorname{index}(p_j)$, one at a time* ⟦Chapter10/SublevelCW · isFiniteCellComplexType_sublevel_of_isCompact⟧.

*Proof as formalized.* The critical points in $N^{a_0}$ form a compact set (the critical set is closed ⟦Chapter1/ManifoldMorse · isClosed_setOf_isCriticalPointM⟧ and $N^{a_0}$ is compact) which is discrete, non-degenerate critical points being isolated ⟦Chapter1/ManifoldMorse · isDiscrete_inter_setOf_isCriticalPointM⟧; so it is finite. The statement is then proved for every level $a \le a_0$ that is not a critical value, by strong induction on the number of critical points below $a$.

- If there is one, let $c$ be the largest critical value below $a$ and $S$ the critical points at level $c$. A level $a' < c$ above all smaller critical values is chosen (the midpoint between $c$ and the next critical value below it, or $c - 1$ if there is none); it is not a critical value, every critical point outside $S$ lies below $a'$ or above $a$, and there are fewer critical points below $a'$ than below $a$. The induction hypothesis at $a'$ and the step above at the level $c$ give the claim at $a$; the list is the one for $a'$ followed by the points of $S$, which keeps it sorted.
- If there is none, $N^a$ is empty: otherwise the compact $N^a$ has a minimum point $q_0$ of $f$, which is a global minimum (outside $N^a$, $f > a \ge f(q_0)$), hence a critical point ⟦Chapter2/CWType · isCriticalPointM_of_forall_le⟧, and below $a$ because $a$ is not a critical value. The empty space has the empty cell list ⟦Chapter2/CWType · IsFiniteCellComplexType.of_isEmpty⟧.

This is the induction of Part II's Theorem 3.5 for regular sublevel sets ⟦Chapter2/CWType · isFiniteCellComplexType_sublevel'⟧, without compactness of $N$ and with non-degeneracy required only in $N^{a_0}$.

**The pieces for $E'$.** *For every $b$, $\{y \in U : E'(y) < b\} = N^m \cap O$ for an open $O$* ⟦Chapter10/Finite · BrokenSetup.exists_isOpen_sublevel_lt⟧, from $U = N^m \cap O_0$ and the continuity of $E'$ on $U$. *For $b < c$ there is a smooth $F$ on $V^m$ with $F = E'$ on $B^b$* ⟦Chapter10/Finite · BrokenSetup.exists_contDiff_eq_energy'⟧, by the extension lemma with $K = B^b$ (compact, VIII.4), $S' = U$, and the local smooth representatives of VIII.3. And the index of a broken geodesic is recorded as a natural number ⟦Chapter10/Finite · BrokenSetup.geodIndex⟧: the index of $E_{**}$ at $\omega_y$, which is finite at a geodesic by Theorem 16.2, and $0$ for $y \notin U$.

**Theorem 16.3 in the model (V10.12).** *Given Milnor's data and $a < c$, suppose that $E'(y) \ne a$ for every $y \in U$ with $\omega_y$ a geodesic, and that $E_{**}$ has nullity $0$ at every geodesic $\omega_y$, $y \in U$, with $E'(y) \le a$. Then there is a list of break points $y^{(1)}, \dots, y^{(j)}$ without repetitions whose members are exactly the $y \in U$ with $\omega_y$ an unbroken geodesic and $E'(y) \le a$; each carries as recorded index the index of $E_{**}$ at $\omega_y$; the list is sorted by energy; and $B^a = \{y \in U : E'(y) \le a\}$ has the homotopy type of a finite cell complex with one cell of dimension $\operatorname{index}(\omega_{y^{(i)}})$ for each $i$.* ⟦Chapter10/Finite · BrokenSetup.theorem_16_3⟧

*Proof as formalized.*

1. Let $c_1 = (a + c)/2$. Take $F$ smooth on $V^m$ with $F = E'$ on $B^{c_1}$, and the open $O$ with $\{y \in U : E' < c_1\} = N^m \cap O$. The manifold $S'' = N^m \cap O$ is a slice submanifold, contained in $U$, on which $F = E'$.
2. The sublevel set of $F|S''$ at $a$ is $B^a$, since $a < c_1$; it is compact by VIII.4.
3. The critical points of $F|S''$ are exactly the $y$ with $\omega_y$ an unbroken geodesic (VIII.5). At those with $F(y) \le a$ the nullity is that of $E_{**}$ (VIII.7), which is $0$, so they are non-degenerate ⟦Chapter1/Nondegenerate · isNondegenerate_iff_nullity_eq_zero⟧. The level $a$ is not a critical value, by hypothesis.
4. Theorem 3.5 for a compact sublevel set applies with $a_0 = a$, and the index of each critical point is the index of $E_{**}$ at the geodesic (VIII.7).
5. The cell structure is transported along the evident homeomorphism between $B^a \subseteq V^m$ and the sublevel set of $F|S''$ at $a$ in $S''$.

**Counting, and Milnor's hypothesis.** The same statement in counted form ⟦Chapter10/Finite · BrokenSetup.theorem_16_3_count⟧: the set of break points of geodesics with $E' \le a$ is finite, and the number of cells of each dimension $\lambda$ is the number of these geodesics of index $\lambda$. With Milnor's hypothesis ⟦Chapter10/Finite · BrokenSetup.theorem_16_3_nonconj⟧: if $0$ and $1$ are not conjugate along any geodesic $\omega_y$ with $E'(y) \le a$, the nullity there is $0$, since by Theorem 14.1 the nullity is the multiplicity of conjugacy ⟦Chapter9/JacobiSpace · theorem_14_1_nullity⟧ and a positive multiplicity means conjugate ⟦Chapter9/JacobiSpace · isConjugate_iff⟧.

**The hypothesis on $a$.** Milnor's non-conjugacy hypothesis does not exclude a geodesic of energy exactly $a$; then $a$ is a critical value of $E'$ and Theorem 3.5 does not describe $B^a$ directly. The formal statements assume that $a$ is not the energy of a geodesic, which is narrower than the book; the critical level is not formalized (V10.12).

### VIII.9 Milnor's Remark on p. 92: a minimum of $E'$

*For every $\omega \in \Omega$ with $E(\omega) < c$, the break points of $\omega$ lie in $U$ and $E'(\omega(t_1), \dots, \omega(t_m)) \le E(\omega)$* ⟦Chapter10/Finite · BrokenSetup.nodeVal_mem_nodes_of_energy_lt⟧ (V10.14): the inequality is the minimality of VIII.6, and the distances are finite because each is at most the length of a piece of $\omega$.

**The Remark (V10.14).** *If some $\gamma_0 \in \Omega$ has $E(\gamma_0) < c$, there is $y \in U$ such that $\omega_y$ is an unbroken geodesic and $E(\omega_y) \le E(\gamma)$ for every $\gamma \in \Omega$.* ⟦Chapter10/Finite · BrokenSetup.exists_energy_minimizing⟧

*Proof as formalized.* Let $a = E(\gamma_0)$. The set $B^a$ is compact (VIII.4), non-empty because it contains the break points of $\gamma_0$, and $E'$ is continuous on it (VIII.3), so $E'$ attains its minimum on $B^a$ at some $y$ ⟦Mathlib/Topology/Order/Compact · IsCompact.exists_isMinOn⟧. This minimum is global over $\Omega$. For $\gamma \in \Omega$ with $E(\gamma) < c$: if $E'$ of its break points is $\le a$, then $E'(y) \le E'(\gamma(t_1), \dots) \le E(\gamma)$; otherwise $E'(y) \le a < E'(\gamma(t_1), \dots) \le E(\gamma)$. If $E(\gamma) \ge c$, then $E'(y) \le a < c \le E(\gamma)$. Since $E(\omega_y) = E'(y)$, $\omega_y$ minimizes $E$ on $\Omega$. To see that it is a geodesic, use Corollary 12.3: it suffices that $\omega_y$ is a critical path ⟦Chapter8/Critical · IsCriticalPath⟧, that is, that along every variation $\alpha$ of $\omega_y$ the derivative of $u \mapsto E(\alpha(u))$ at $0$, which exists ⟦Chapter8/FirstVariation · IsVariation.hasDerivAt_energy⟧, vanishes. The paths $\alpha(u)$ lie in $\Omega$, so $u \mapsto E(\alpha(u))$ has a local minimum at $0$, and its derivative there is $0$.

Milnor calls the minimum the required minimal geodesic, a second proof of the existence part of Hopf–Rinow. The formal statement proves minimality of the energy on $\Omega$; that $\omega_y$ is also shortest needs a reparametrization by arc length and is not stated. Nothing beyond Milnor's data and one path of energy $< c$ is assumed.

### VIII.10 Milnor's metric on the path space

So far $\Omega$ has been a set. Milnor topologizes it (p. 88) by the distance
$$
d(\omega, \omega') = \max_{0 \le t \le 1} \rho(\omega(t), \omega'(t)) + \Big[\int_0^1 \Big(\frac{ds}{dt} - \frac{ds'}{dt}\Big)^2 dt\Big]^{1/2},
$$
$s$, $s'$ the arc lengths, and adds the second term so that the energy becomes continuous.

**Two integral inequalities.** *Cauchy–Schwarz for interval integrals*: $(\int_a^b fg)^2 \le \int_a^b f^2 \int_a^b g^2$ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · integral_mul_sq_le⟧. For every real $x$, $0 \le \int_a^b (f - xg)^2$, a quadratic in $x$ whose discriminant must therefore be $\le 0$ ⟦Mathlib/Algebra/QuadraticDiscriminant · discrim_le_zero⟧. *Minkowski's inequality*: $\big(\int (f + g)^2\big)^{1/2} \le \big(\int f^2\big)^{1/2} + \big(\int g^2\big)^{1/2}$ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · sqrt_integral_add_sq_le⟧, by expanding the square and bounding the cross term with Cauchy–Schwarz.

**Velocities of piecewise smooth paths.** On each open piece the velocity of a piecewise smooth path agrees with a function continuous on the closed piece, its one-sided derivative within the piece ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · IsPiecewiseSmooth.exists_deriv_piece⟧ ⟦Chapter8/Energy · IsPiecewiseSmooth.derivWithin_piece⟧. Hence a continuous function of the velocities of two, or three, piecewise smooth paths is integrable on every subinterval ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · intervalIntegrable_comp_deriv⟧ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · intervalIntegrable_comp_deriv₃⟧: take the union of the break sets and integrate piece by piece ⟦Chapter8/Piecewise · intervalIntegrable_of_piecewise⟧. And *the speed of a piecewise smooth path on $[a, b]$ is bounded* ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · IsPiecewiseSmooth.exists_bound_deriv⟧: on each piece the one-sided derivative is continuous on a compact interval, the bounds of adjacent pieces combine by taking the maximum ⟦Chapter8/Piecewise · piecewise_induction⟧, and at a point where the path is not differentiable the formal derivative is $0$.

**The space.** $\Omega$ becomes a type of its own, its points the maps $\mathbb R \to V$ in $\Omega$ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp⟧ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.mem⟧, with speed $\dot s(t) = \|\omega'(t)\|$ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.speed⟧, so that $E_a^b(\omega) = \int_a^b \dot s^2$ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.energy_eq_integral_speed⟧. The uniform part of $d$ is $\sup_{t \in [0, 1]} \rho(\omega(t), \omega'(t))$, taken in $[0, \infty]$ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.supDist⟧, and the $L^2$ part is $\big(\int_0^1 (\dot s - \dot s')^2\big)^{1/2}$ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.l2Dist⟧. The $L^2$ part is nonnegative, symmetric and zero on the diagonal ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.l2Dist_nonneg⟧ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.l2Dist_comm⟧ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.l2Dist_self⟧; continuous functions of two speeds are integrable on subintervals of $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.intervalIntegrable_speed⟧; and the triangle inequality is Minkowski's inequality applied to $(\dot s - \dot s') + (\dot s' - \dot s'')$, the integrability of the cross term coming from the three-path lemma ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.l2Dist_triangle⟧. The uniform part vanishes on the diagonal, the paths lying in $N$ on $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.supDist_self⟧, is symmetric ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.supDist_comm⟧, satisfies the triangle inequality pointwise and hence for the supremum ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.supDist_triangle⟧, and dominates each $\rho(\omega(t), \omega'(t))$ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.riemDist_le_supDist⟧.

**Milnor's metric (V10.1).** *$d = $ uniform part $+$ $L^2$ part is an extended pseudometric on $\Omega$* ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.instPseudoEMetricSpace⟧ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.edist_def⟧ ⟦Mathlib/Topology/EMetricSpace/Defs · PseudoEMetricSpace⟧, with both parts bounded by $d$ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.ofReal_l2Dist_le_edist⟧ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.supDist_le_edist⟧. From now on $\Omega$ and its subsets carry this topology (§0.8). It is a *pseudo*metric: maps that agree on $[0, 1]$ are at distance $0$ ⟦Chapter10/Omega · PathSp.edist_eq_zero_of_eqOn⟧, because both parts see only $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · PathSp.supDist_congr⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · PathSp.l2Dist_congr⟧. On paths $[0, 1] \to N$ it is Milnor's metric. Formally its values lie in $[0, \infty]$, because $\rho$ does.

**Lemma 16.A of the exposition: the energy is continuous (V10.2 (i)).** *For $\omega, \omega' \in \Omega$ and $0 \le a \le b \le 1$,*
$$
\big|\sqrt{E_a^b(\omega)} - \sqrt{E_a^b(\omega')}\big| \le \Big(\int_0^1 (\dot s - \dot s')^2\Big)^{1/2}.
$$
⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.abs_sqrt_energy_sub_le⟧. Write $\dot s = (\dot s - \dot s') + \dot s'$; Minkowski on $[a, b]$ gives $\sqrt{E_a^b(\omega)} \le \|\dot s - \dot s'\|_{L^2[a, b]} + \sqrt{E_a^b(\omega')}$, the first term is at most the $L^2$ part of $d$ because the integral of a nonnegative function grows with the interval, and the roles of $\omega$, $\omega'$ can be exchanged. Consequences: $\sqrt{E_a^b}$ is $1$-Lipschitz for $d$ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.lipschitzWith_sqrt_energy⟧; $E_a^b \ge 0$ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.energy_nonneg_of_le⟧, so $E_a^b$, the square of a continuous function, is continuous ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.continuous_energy⟧; $\Omega^c$ is closed ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.isClosed_sublevel⟧ and $\operatorname{Int}\Omega^c = E^{-1}[0, c)$ is open ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.isOpen_sublevel_lt⟧. This is what Milnor's $L^2$ term is for: with the uniform term alone, a small wiggle of high frequency added to a path converges uniformly while its energy blows up.

**Lemma 16.B of the exposition: no energy in short intervals (V10.2 (ii)).** *If $\omega_n \to \omega$ in $\Omega$, then for every $\eta > 0$ there is $\delta > 0$ such that $E_a^b(\omega_n) < \eta$ for all $n$ and all $0 \le a \le b \le 1$ with $b - a < \delta$.* ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.exists_energy_lt_of_tendsto⟧

*Proof as formalized.* Choose $n_0$ with $L^2$ distance from $\omega_n$ to $\omega$ less than $\sqrt\eta/2$ for $n \ge n_0$. The speeds of $\omega$ and of $\omega_0, \dots, \omega_{n_0 - 1}$ are bounded; let $K$ be the sum of the bounds, so each of these paths has $E_a^b \le K^2(b - a)$ ⟦Chapter10/PathMetric · PathSp.energy_le_of_bound⟧. Take $\delta = \eta/(4(K^2 + 1))$, so that $K^2(b - a) < \eta/4$. For $n < n_0$, $E_a^b(\omega_n) < \eta/4$. For $n \ge n_0$, $\sqrt{E_a^b(\omega)} < \sqrt\eta/2$, and Lemma 16.A gives $\sqrt{E_a^b(\omega_n)} < \sqrt\eta/2 + \sqrt\eta/2$. Milnor does not state this lemma; the continuity of $r_u$ needs it.

### VIII.11 Milnor's deformation $r_u$

Two lemmas on the energy: on $[a, b]$ it depends only on the path on $[a, b]$, the derivatives agreeing on the open interval ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · energy_congr_Icc⟧; and *Schwarz's inequality with the distance*, $\rho(\omega(a), \omega(b))^2 \le (b - a)E_a^b(\omega)$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · riemDist_sq_le_energy⟧, from $\rho \le L$ and $L^2 \le (b-a)E$.

**The short geodesic over an interval.** For $x, x'$ and $a < b$, $\sigma(\tau) = \exp_x\big(\frac{\tau - a}{b - a}\, v(x, x')\big)$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.sgeoOver⟧ runs from $x$ at $\tau = a$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.sgeoOver_left⟧ to $x'$ at $\tau = b$ when $x, x' \in S$ and $\rho(x, x') < \delta$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.sgeoOver_right⟧. It is then a geodesic on a neighbourhood of $[a, b]$ with speed $\|v(x, x')\|/(b - a)$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.sgeoOver_isGeodesicOn⟧, proved like the segments of VIII.2, and its energy over $[a, b]$ is $\rho(x, x')^2/(b - a)$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.energy_sgeoOver⟧.

**Definition.** For a segment $i$, a number $u$ and a path $\omega$, the path $r^{(i)}_u(\omega)$ is ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.retrSeg⟧
$$
r^{(i)}_u(\omega)(\tau) = \begin{cases} \omega_y(\tau), & \tau \le t_i, \\ \exp_{\omega(t_i)}\big(\frac{\tau - t_i}{u - t_i}\, v(\omega(t_i), \omega(u))\big), & t_i < \tau \le u, \\ \omega(\tau), & \tau > u, \end{cases}
$$
with $y = (\omega(t_1), \dots, \omega(t_m))$ the break points of $\omega$; and Milnor's $r_u(\omega)$ is $r^{(i)}_u(\omega)$ for the segment $i$ of $u$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.retr⟧. On $[0, t_i]$ the formal definition uses the broken geodesic of *all* break points of $\omega$, which on $[0, t_i]$ is Milnor's $r(\omega)$.

Standing hypotheses: $\omega \in \Omega$, $E(\omega) < c$, and $u \in [t_i, t_{i+1}]$, which lies in $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.u_mem⟧. Then:

- *Two points of $\omega$ in one segment are at distance $< \delta$* ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.riemDist_lt_of_seg⟧: $\rho^2 \le (b - a)E_a^b(\omega) \le \ell_i\,c < \delta^2$. So the short geodesic in the middle formula exists: Milnor's $\rho(\omega(t_{i-1}), \omega(u))^2 \le (u - t_{i-1})E < \varepsilon^2$.
- The break points of $\omega$ lie in $U$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.nodeVal_mem⟧ (VIII.9), and the broken geodesic of the break points passes through the vertices ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.path_t⟧, which are the points $\omega(t_k)$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.path_nodeVal_t⟧; the energy of its $k$-th segment is $\rho(x_k, x_{k+1})^2/\ell_k$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.energy_path_seg⟧.
- *The three pieces.* $r^{(i)}_u(\omega)$ is the broken geodesic of the break points on $(-\infty, t_i]$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.retrSeg_eqOn_left⟧; the short geodesic from $\omega(t_i)$ to $\omega(u)$ over $[t_i, u]$ on the *closed* interval $[t_i, u]$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.retrSeg_eqOn_mid⟧, because at $\tau = t_i$ the first formula gives $\omega(t_i)$, the start of the short geodesic; and $\omega$ on $[u, \infty)$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.retrSeg_eqOn_right⟧, because at $\tau = u$ the middle formula gives $\omega(u)$ (and, when $u = t_i$, the first formula gives $\omega(t_i)$).
- *$r^{(i)}_u(\omega) \in \Omega$* ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.retrSeg_mem_pathSpace⟧: it is the concatenation ⟦Chapter7/Path · IsPiecewiseSmooth.concat⟧ of three piecewise smooth pieces that match at $t_i$ and at $u$; the middle piece is a smooth geodesic ⟦Chapter7/Path · IsPiecewiseSmooth.of_contDiffOn⟧, or a single point when $u = t_i$; all values lie in $N$; it starts at $\omega_y(0) = p$ and ends at $\omega(1) = q$ (at the short geodesic's end $\omega(1)$ when $u = 1$).
- *The energy does not increase* (Milnor p. 91). On $[t_i, u]$, $E(r^{(i)}_u(\omega)) = \rho(\omega(t_i), \omega(u))^2/(u - t_i) \le E_{t_i}^u(\omega)$ by Schwarz, both sides being $0$ when $u = t_i$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.energy_retrSeg_mid_le⟧. Overall ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.energy_retrSeg_le⟧, split both energies over the segments: for $k < i$ the path is the broken geodesic there, with energy $\rho(\omega(t_k), \omega(t_{k+1}))^2/\ell_k \le E_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}}(\omega)$; for $k = i$ split once more at $u$, the middle part decreasing and the rest equal; for $k > i$ the path is $\omega$.

**The formulas of neighbouring segments agree.** On a segment $[t_k, t_{k+1}]$ the broken geodesic of the break points of $\omega$ is the short geodesic from $\omega(t_k)$ to $\omega(t_{k+1})$ over $[t_k, t_{k+1}]$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.path_nodeVal_eq_sgeoOver⟧. *Hence for every $u \in [t_i, t_{i+1}]$, $r^{(i)}_u(\omega) = r_u(\omega)$* ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.retrSeg_eq_retr⟧. For $u < t_{i+1}$ the segment of $u$ is $i$, and for $u = t_{i+1} = 1$ it is the last one, which is $i$. For an interior $u = t_{i+1}$ the segment of $u$ is $i + 1$, and the formula of segment $i + 1$ with $u = t_{i+1}$ is the broken geodesic on $(-\infty, t_{i+1}]$ and $\omega$ beyond; the formula of segment $i$ is the broken geodesic on $(-\infty, t_i]$, the short geodesic over $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$, and $\omega$ beyond, and the short geodesic is the broken geodesic on that segment. This is the agreement at $u = t_i$ that the exposition notes Milnor leaves implicit; it is what makes $r_u$ well defined as a function of $u$, and what lets the continuity, proved segment by segment, glue (VIII.13).

**$r_u$ for $u \in [0, 1]$** (V10.7). *$r_u(\omega) \in \Omega$* ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.retr_mem_pathSpace⟧ and *$E(r_u(\omega)) \le E(\omega)$* ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.energy_retr_le⟧, from the segment of $u$. *$r_0$ is the identity on $[0, 1]$* ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.retr_zero⟧: the segment of $0$ is the first, $t_0 = 0$, and $r_0(\omega)$ is $\omega_y(0) = \omega(0)$ at $\tau = 0$ and $\omega(\tau)$ for $\tau > 0$. *$r_1$ is Milnor's retraction $r$*: $r_1(\omega)$ is the broken geodesic of the break points of $\omega$ on $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.retr_one⟧, since the segment of $1$ is the last, its middle formula is the short geodesic over $[t_m, 1]$, and that is the broken geodesic there.

**$r_u$ fixes $B$.** *For $\gamma \in B$ and $u \in [0, 1]$, $r_u(\gamma) = \gamma$ on $[0, 1]$* ⟦Chapter10/Retraction · BrokenSetup.retr_of_mem_brokenPaths⟧. For $\tau \le t_i$ this is Lemma 16.1 (b). For $t_i < \tau \le u$, take $w$ from Step 3 of VIII.2 on segment $i$ and $s = (u - t_i)/\ell_i \in (0, 1]$. Then $s\,w$ is tangent, $\|s\,w\| \le \|w\| < \delta$, and $\exp_{\gamma(t_i)}(s\,w) = \gamma(u)$; by uniqueness $v(\gamma(t_i), \gamma(u)) = s\,w$, and
$$
\exp_{\gamma(t_i)}\Big(\frac{\tau - t_i}{u - t_i}\, s\,w\Big) = \exp_{\gamma(t_i)}\Big(\frac{\tau - t_i}{\ell_i}\, w\Big) = \gamma(\tau).
$$
For $\tau > u$ there is nothing to prove. Milnor's tacit reason is that a sub-arc of a short geodesic is the short geodesic between its own ends, which is the uniqueness in Corollary 10.8.

### VIII.12 The continuity of $r_u$ in both variables

Milnor says that $r_u(\omega)$ is continuous in both variables, and that "it is easily verified" (p. 91). The formal proof fixes a segment $i$ and, along an arbitrary filter, numbers $u_j \to u$ in $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$ and paths $\omega_j \to \omega$ in $\Omega$, all of energy $< c$, and shows that $d(r^{(i)}_{u_j}(\omega_j), r^{(i)}_u(\omega)) \to 0$, the uniform part and the $L^2$ part separately.

**Ingredients.**

- *The domain of $\exp$ is star-shaped along $[0, 1]$* ⟦Chapter10/RetractionCont · mem_expDom_smul⟧: if $\exp_x(v)$ is defined, so is $\exp_x(\theta v)$ for $\theta \in [0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter7/Exp · IsGeodesicFrom.exp_smul⟧.
- *Convergence for $\rho$ implies convergence in $V$* ⟦Chapter10/RetractionCont · tendsto_of_tendsto_riemDist⟧, since $\|x - y\| \le \rho(x, y)$; the converse holds within $N$ ⟦Chapter7/Metric · tendsto_riemDist_of_tendsto⟧.
- *The short geodesics depend continuously on their end points.* On the set of *joinable* pairs, pairs of points of $S$ at distance $< \delta$ ⟦Chapter10/RetractionCont · BrokenSetup.joinable⟧, the join vector is continuous ⟦Chapter10/RetractionCont · BrokenSetup.continuousWithinAt_joinVec⟧, because near a joinable pair it agrees with the smooth map $G$ of Corollary 10.8 (uniqueness again). And $(x, x', \theta) \mapsto \exp_x(\theta\,v(x, x'))$ is continuous on joinable pairs times $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter10/RetractionCont · BrokenSetup.continuousWithinAt_shortGeo⟧, because $\exp$ is continuous on its domain, which is open in $TN$ ⟦Chapter7/GeodesicFlow · eventually_mem_expDom_and_continuousWithinAt_exp⟧, and $(x, \theta v)$ lies in the domain with $\theta v$ tangent.
- *Paths of bounded energy are equicontinuous*: $\rho(\omega(s), \omega(\tau)) \le \sqrt{|\tau - s|\,E(\omega)}$ ⟦Chapter10/RetractionCont · PathSp.riemDist_le_sqrt⟧, from $\rho \le L$ and $L^2 \le |\tau - s|E_s^\tau \le |\tau - s|E$. Hence *$\omega_j(\sigma_j) \to \omega(\sigma)$ for $\rho$ when $\omega_j \to \omega$ and $\sigma_j \to \sigma$ in $[0, 1]$* ⟦Chapter10/RetractionCont · PathSp.tendsto_riemDist_apply⟧: $\rho(\omega_j(\sigma_j), \omega(\sigma)) \le d(\omega_j, \omega) + \sqrt{|\sigma - \sigma_j|E(\omega)}$.
- *Lemma 16.B along a filter* ⟦Chapter10/RetractionCont · PathSp.eventually_energy_lt⟧: if $\omega_j \to \omega$, there is $\delta > 0$ such that eventually $E_a^b(\omega_j) < \eta$ whenever $b - a < \delta$; the proof of VIII.10 with a bound for the speed of $\omega$ alone.
- *$L^2$ bookkeeping.* Speeds agree inside an interval where the paths agree ⟦Chapter10/RetractionCont · PathSp.speed_congr⟧; the $L^2$ distance of the speeds over a subinterval is at most the full one ⟦Chapter10/RetractionCont · PathSp.integral_speed_sub_sq_le⟧; and $\int_a^b(\dot s - \dot s')^2 \le 2E_a^b(\omega) + 2E_a^b(\omega')$ ⟦Chapter10/RetractionCont · PathSp.integral_speed_sub_sq_le_energy⟧, from $(x - y)^2 \le 2x^2 + 2y^2$.
- *The speeds of broken geodesics depend continuously on the break points.* For $y, y' \in U$, $\int_0^1 (\dot s_{y'} - \dot s_y)^2 = \sum_k (\rho'_k - \rho_k)^2/\ell_k$ ⟦Chapter10/RetractionCont · BrokenSetup.integral_speed_path_sub_sq⟧, the speeds being the constants $\rho_k/\ell_k$ on the segments; so if $y_j \to y$ in $U$ the integral tends to $0$ ⟦Chapter10/RetractionCont · BrokenSetup.tendsto_integral_speed_path⟧, the gaps being continuous and finite at $y$.
- *Uniform convergence from pointwise convergence* ⟦Chapter10/RetractionConv · PathSp.eventually_supDist_le_of_tendsto_apply⟧: if $\alpha_j(s) \to \alpha(s)$ for every $s \in [0, 1]$ and all energies are $\le C$, then eventually $\sup_s \rho(\alpha_j(s), \alpha(s)) \le e$. With $h = (e/3)^2/(C + 1)$ and a finite $h$-net of $[0, 1]$ ⟦Mathlib/Topology/MetricSpace/Pseudo/Basic · finite_cover_balls_of_compact⟧, eventually $\rho(\alpha_j(g), \alpha(g)) \le e/3$ on the net, equicontinuity gives $\rho(\beta(s), \beta(\tau)) \le e/3$ for $|s - \tau| < h$ and every $\beta$ of energy $\le C$, and three thirds add up. Paths of energy $< c$ are uniformly $\frac12$-Hölder for $\rho$; that makes the uniform part easy.
- *Short geodesics with converging data converge* ⟦Chapter10/RetractionConv · BrokenSetup.tendsto_sgeoOver⟧: if $x_j \to x$, $x'_j \to x'$ (joinable pairs), $b_j \to b > a$ and $\tau_j \to \tau$, $\tau_j \in [a, b_j]$, the short geodesic from $x_j$ to $x'_j$ over $[a, b_j]$ at $\tau_j$ tends to the limit one at $\tau$, since $(\tau_j - a)/(b_j - a) \to (\tau - a)/(b - a) \in [0, 1]$. Points of one segment of a path of energy $< c$ are joinable ⟦Chapter10/RetractionConv · BrokenSetup.mem_joinable⟧.
- The speed of $r^{(i)}_u(\omega)$ on $(t_i, u)$ is $\|v(\omega(t_i), \omega(u))\|/(u - t_i)$ ⟦Chapter10/RetractionConv · BrokenSetup.speed_retrSeg_mid⟧, and its energy on $[s, t] \subseteq [t_i, u]$ is that speed squared times $t - s$ ⟦Chapter10/RetractionConv · BrokenSetup.energy_retrSeg_mid⟧. If $\omega_j \to \omega$, the $L^2$ distance tends to $0$ ⟦Chapter10/RetractionConv · BrokenSetup.tendsto_l2Dist⟧, $\omega_j(\sigma_j) \to \omega(\sigma)$ in $V$ ⟦Chapter10/RetractionConv · BrokenSetup.tendsto_apply⟧, and the break points converge ⟦Chapter10/RetractionConv · BrokenSetup.tendsto_nodeVal⟧.

**Pointwise convergence.** *For every $s \in [0, 1]$, $r^{(i)}_{u_j}(\omega_j)(s) \to r^{(i)}_u(\omega)(s)$ in $V$* ⟦Chapter10/RetractionConv · BrokenSetup.tendsto_retrSeg_apply⟧. Four cases.

1. $s \le t_i$: on the segment $k$ of $s$ all the paths are short geodesics over the fixed interval $[t_k, t_{k+1}]$ between the converging points $\omega_j(t_k) \to \omega(t_k)$ and $\omega_j(t_{k+1}) \to \omega(t_{k+1})$.
2. $t_i < s < u$: eventually $s < u_j$, and all are middle formulas, short geodesics with ends $\omega_j(t_i) \to \omega(t_i)$ and $\omega_j(u_j) \to \omega(u)$ over $[t_i, u_j]$, $u_j \to u > t_i$.
3. $s = u > t_i$: the limit is $\omega(u)$. The value $r^{(i)}_{u_j}(\omega_j)(s)$ is the short geodesic at $\min(s, u_j)$ when $s \le u_j$, which tends to the end $\omega(u)$ of the limit geodesic, and $\omega_j(s)$ otherwise, which tends to $\omega(s) = \omega(u)$; both branches converge to the same limit.
4. $s > u$: eventually $s > u_j$, and the values are $\omega_j(s) \to \omega(s)$.

**Uniform convergence.** *Eventually $\sup_s \rho\big(r^{(i)}_{u_j}(\omega_j)(s), r^{(i)}_u(\omega)(s)\big) \le e$* ⟦Chapter10/RetractionConv · BrokenSetup.eventually_supDist_retrSeg_le⟧: all these paths lie in $\Omega$ with energy $\le c$ (VIII.11), so pointwise convergence is uniform.

**Convergence of the speeds in $L^2$.** *Eventually $\int_0^1 (\dot s_j - \dot s)^2 \le e$, with $\dot s_j$, $\dot s$ the speeds of $r^{(i)}_{u_j}(\omega_j)$ and $r^{(i)}_u(\omega)$* ⟦Chapter10/RetractionConv · BrokenSetup.eventually_integral_retrSeg_le⟧. This is the delicate part, and the one where Milnor's two terms of $d$ really interact. The integral is split at $t_i$.

- *On $[0, t_i]$* both paths are broken geodesics of the break points of $\omega_j$ and of $\omega$, and the break points converge, so the integral is eventually $\le e/4$.
- *Beyond $\max(u_j, u)$* the paths are $\omega_j$ and $\omega$, so the integral there is at most the square of the $L^2$ distance of $\omega_j$ and $\omega$, eventually $\le e/4$.
- *On $[t_i, 1]$ when $u = t_i$.* The short geodesic over $[t_i, u_j]$ may be arbitrarily fast as $u_j \to t_i$, so its speed is not controlled; its energy is. Split at $u_j$. On $[t_i, u_j]$, the integral is at most $2E(r^{(i)}_{u_j}(\omega_j)) + 2E(r^{(i)}_u(\omega))$ over that interval, which is at most $2E_{t_i}^{u_j}(\omega_j) + 2E_{t_i}^{u_j}(\omega)$, because the middle energy decreases and $r^{(i)}_u(\omega) = \omega$ past $u = t_i$; since $u_j - t_i \to 0$, Lemma 16.B along the filter, for $(\omega_j)$ and for the constant family $\omega$, makes each energy $< e/16$, so the sum is $< e/4$. The tail beyond $u_j$ contributes $\le e/4$. This is the $L^2$ speed control: the contribution of a fast short geodesic is bounded by its energy $\rho(\omega(t_i), \omega(u))^2/(u - t_i) \le E_{t_i}^u(\omega)$, which is uniformly small for paths near a given one.
- *On $[t_i, 1]$ when $t_i < u$.* Split at $\min(u_j, u)$ and $\max(u_j, u)$. On $[t_i, \min]$ both paths are short geodesics of constant speeds $\sigma_j = \|v(\omega_j(t_i), \omega_j(u_j))\|/(u_j - t_i)$ and $\sigma$, and $\sigma_j \to \sigma$ by the continuity of the join vector and $u - t_i > 0$; so the integral is at most $(\sigma_j - \sigma)^2 \le e/8$ eventually, the interval having length $\le 1$. On $[\min, \max]$, an interval of length $|u_j - u| \to 0$, the integral is at most $2E + 2E$: the energy of $r^{(i)}_u(\omega)$ there is $< e/32$ by Lemma 16.B for that fixed path; the energy of $r^{(i)}_{u_j}(\omega_j)$ is $< e/32$ by Lemma 16.B for $(\omega_j)$ if $u_j \le u$ (the path is $\omega_j$ there), and is $\sigma_j^2(u_j - u) \le e/32$ eventually if $u < u_j$ (the path is the short geodesic there). So the middle part is $\le e/8$, and the tail beyond $\max$ is $\le e/4$.

In both cases $[t_i, 1]$ contributes $\le e/2 \le 3e/4$, and with $[0, t_i]$ the total is $\le e$.

**Continuity on a segment.** *$d(r^{(i)}_{u_j}(\omega_j), r^{(i)}_u(\omega)) \to 0$* ⟦Chapter10/RetractionConv · BrokenSetup.tendsto_retrSeg⟧: given $\varepsilon$, choose $0 < r < \varepsilon$; eventually the uniform part is $\le r/2$ and the integral is $\le (r/2)^2$, so the $L^2$ part is $\le r/2$.

### VIII.13 $\Omega^a$ deformation retracts onto $B^a$

Paths are maps on $\mathbb R$, and $r_u(\omega)$ agrees with Milnor's only on $[0, 1]$. To obtain a self-map of $\Omega$ for which $r_0 = \mathrm{id}$ holds exactly and $r_u$ fixes $B$ exactly, $r_u(\omega)$ is completed by $\omega$ off $[0, 1]$. Membership in $\Omega$ and both parts of $d$ depend only on $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · PathSp.pathSpace_congr⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · PathSp.supDist_congr⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · PathSp.l2Dist_congr⟧, and a map at distance $0$ from a continuous map is continuous ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · PathSp.continuous_of_edist_eq_zero⟧.

**The deformation as a self-map of $\Omega$.** For $u \in [0, 1]$ and $E(\omega) < c$, $\bar r_u(\omega)$ is $r_u(\omega)$ on $[0, 1]$ and $\omega$ off $[0, 1]$; otherwise it is $\omega$ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.deformFun⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.deformFun_mem⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.deform⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.coe_deform⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.deform_of_not⟧. On $[0, 1]$ it is $r_u(\omega)$ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.eqOn_deform⟧, so the distance between two of its values is the distance between the corresponding values of $r$ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.edist_deform⟧. The following hold exactly, not only on $[0, 1]$: $\bar r_0 = \mathrm{id}$ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.deform_zero⟧; $\bar r_u$ fixes every point of $B$ for $u \in [0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.deform_of_mem_brokenPaths⟧; $E(\bar r_u(\omega)) \le E(\omega)$ for all $u$ and $\omega$ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.energy_deform_le⟧ ⟦Chapter8/Hessian · energy_congr⟧; and *$\bar r_1$ retracts $\operatorname{Int}\Omega^c$ into $B$* ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.deform_one_mem_brokenPaths⟧: on $[0, 1]$, $\bar r_1(\omega)$ is the broken geodesic of the break points of $\omega$, which lies in $B$ by Lemma 16.1, and both the broken geodesic property and the energy pass along agreement on $[0, 1]$.

**Lemma 16.C of the exposition: continuity in both variables (V10.7).** *$(u, \omega) \mapsto \bar r_u(\omega)$ is continuous on $[0, 1] \times \operatorname{Int}\Omega^c$ for Milnor's metric.* ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.continuousOn_deform⟧

*Proof as formalized.* At $(u_0, \omega_0)$, it suffices to show, for each segment $k$, that eventually (in the neighbourhood filter within the domain) $u \in [t_k, t_{k+1}]$ implies $d(\bar r_u(\omega), \bar r_{u_0}(\omega_0)) \le \varepsilon$. Then, since every $u \in [0, 1]$ lies in its own segment and there are finitely many segments, the claim follows.

- If $u_0 \in [t_k, t_{k+1}]$, restrict the filter to $u \in [t_k, t_{k+1}]$ and apply the continuity on a segment (VIII.12) to the two projections. The distance between the values is the distance between $r^{(k)}_u(\omega)$ and $r^{(k)}_{u_0}(\omega_0)$, because $r^{(k)}$ is $r$ on the closed segment, at both points (VIII.11). This is where the agreement at the division points is used: when $u_0 = t_k$, the nearby $u$ lie in segment $k - 1$ or segment $k$, and both formulas give $r_{u_0}$ at $u_0$.
- If $u_0 \notin [t_k, t_{k+1}]$, then eventually $u \notin [t_k, t_{k+1}]$, the complement of a closed interval being open, and the implication is vacuous.

**Theorem 16.2, deformation retraction (V10.8).** *For $a < c$: $(u, \omega) \mapsto \bar r_u(\omega)$ is continuous on $[0, 1] \times \Omega^a$; it does not increase the energy, so each $\bar r_u$ maps $\Omega^a$ into itself; $\bar r_0 = \mathrm{id}$; $\bar r_1$ maps $\Omega^a$ into $B$, hence into $B^a = \Omega^a \cap B$; and $\bar r_u$ fixes every point of $B$ for $u \in [0, 1]$.* ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.theorem_16_2_deformationRetract⟧

So $B^a$ is a strong deformation retract of $\Omega^a$ (§0.4). The continuity on $[0, 1] \times \operatorname{Int}\Omega^c$ and the same identities make $B$ a strong deformation retract of $\operatorname{Int}\Omega^c$, Milnor's footnote. In homotopy terms ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.homotopyEquivBroken⟧ ⟦Mathlib/Topology/Homotopy/Equiv · ContinuousMap.HomotopyEquiv⟧: Milnor's retraction $r = \bar r_1$, as a continuous map $\Omega^a \to B^a$ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.retrMap⟧, and the inclusion $B^a \to \Omega^a$ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.inclMap⟧ are inverse homotopy equivalences, $B^a$ and $\Omega^a$ being subspaces of $\Omega$. The homotopy from the inclusion composed with $r$ to the identity of $\Omega^a$ is $(t, \omega) \mapsto \bar r_{1-t}(\omega)$, continuous with values in $\Omega^a$ ⟦Chapter10/Deformation · BrokenSetup.continuous_deform_sublevel⟧; and $r$ composed with the inclusion is the identity of $B^a$ exactly, because $\bar r_1$ fixes $B$.

### VIII.14 The two descriptions of $B^a$, and Theorem 16.3 for $\Omega^a$

Milnor says (p. 89) that $\omega \mapsto (\omega(t_1), \dots, \omega(t_{k-1}))$ evidently defines a homeomorphism of $B$ onto an open subset of the product. Both directions need an argument for the metric $d$.

- *$y \mapsto \omega_y(s)$ is continuous on $U$, for each $s \in [0, 1]$* ⟦Chapter10/Omega · BrokenSetup.tendsto_path_apply⟧: on the segment $k$ of $s$, $\omega_{y'}(s)$ is the short geodesic from $x_k(y')$ to $x_{k+1}(y')$ over $[t_k, t_{k+1}]$, the vertices depend continuously on $y'$, and short geodesics with converging data converge.
- *$y \mapsto \omega_y$ is continuous from $U$ to $\Omega$* ⟦Chapter10/Omega · BrokenSetup.pathP⟧ ⟦Chapter10/Omega · BrokenSetup.continuous_pathP⟧: the uniform part by uniform convergence from pointwise convergence, all energies being $E' < c$; the $L^2$ part by the continuity of the speeds of broken geodesics in their break points; $r/2 + r/2$.
- *$\omega \mapsto (\omega(t_1), \dots, \omega(t_m))$ is continuous from $\Omega$ to $V^m$* ⟦Chapter10/Omega · BrokenSetup.continuous_nodeVal⟧, since $\|x - y\| \le \rho(x, y) \le d$.

These restrict to continuous maps between $B^a$ as a subspace of $\Omega$ and $B^a$ as a subset of $N^m$: the break points ⟦Chapter10/Omega · BrokenSetup.nodeMap⟧, which land in $\{y \in U : E'(y) \le a\}$ by VIII.9, and the broken geodesics ⟦Chapter10/Omega · BrokenSetup.pathMap⟧, which land in $B^a$ by formula $(\ast)$ and Lemma 16.1.

**The correspondence is a homeomorphism up to paths at distance $0$ (V10.5).** *For every $a$, the two descriptions of $B^a$ are homotopy equivalent* ⟦Chapter10/Omega · BrokenSetup.homotopyEquivNodes⟧. Taking break points after the broken geodesic is the identity, exactly (Lemma 16.1 (a)). Taking the broken geodesic after the break points is homotopic to the identity of $B^a \subseteq \Omega$ through the homotopy $H(t, \gamma) = \omega_{(\gamma(t_j))_j}$ for $t < 1$ and $H(1, \gamma) = \gamma$. As a map it jumps at $t = 1$, but it is continuous for the pseudometric: every value $H(t, \gamma)$ is at distance $0$ from $\gamma$, because $\omega_{(\gamma(t_j))_j} = \gamma$ on $[0, 1]$ (Lemma 16.1 (b)), and a map at distance $0$ from the continuous projection $(t, \gamma) \mapsto \gamma$ is continuous. Since paths are maps on $\mathbb R$, Milnor's homeomorphism becomes this homotopy equivalence, which is a homeomorphism once paths that agree on $[0, 1]$ are identified.

**Theorem 16.3 (V10.13).** *Given Milnor's data with $a < c$, suppose that no geodesic $\gamma \in \Omega$ has $E(\gamma) = a$, and that $E_{**}$ has nullity $0$ at every geodesic $\gamma \in \Omega$ with $E(\gamma) \le a$. Then the geodesics in $\Omega^a$ are finitely many, with break points forming a list without repetitions, each recorded with its index; and $\Omega^a = \{\omega \in \Omega : E(\omega) \le a\}$, with Milnor's metric, has the homotopy type of a finite cell complex with one cell of dimension $\lambda$ for each geodesic in $\Omega^a$ of index $\lambda$.* ⟦Chapter10/Omega · BrokenSetup.theorem_16_3_Omega⟧

*Proof as formalized.* The hypotheses transfer to the model through formula $(\ast)$, $E(\omega_y) = E'(y)$. Theorem 16.3 in the model (VIII.8) gives the cell structure of $\{y \in U : E'(y) \le a\}$; it is carried to $B^a \subseteq \Omega$ along the homotopy equivalence of the two descriptions, and to $\Omega^a$ along the homotopy equivalence $\Omega^a \simeq B^a$ of Theorem 16.2. In counted form ⟦Chapter10/Omega · BrokenSetup.theorem_16_3_Omega_count⟧: the number of cells of dimension $\lambda$ is the number of these geodesics of index $\lambda$. By VIII.5 every geodesic in $\Omega^a$ agrees on $[0, 1]$ with some $\omega_y$, $y$ in the list, so one cell for each geodesic means one cell for each class of geodesics that agree on $[0, 1]$, the only sensible count when paths are maps on $\mathbb R$. Milnor's data exist for every $c > a$ when $N$ is closed in $V$, or connected and complete (VIII.1); his finite CW-complex is an iterated adjunction space with arbitrary attaching maps (§0.5); non-conjugacy may replace nullity $0$ by Theorem 14.1; and $a$ must not be the energy of a geodesic, as in VIII.8.
