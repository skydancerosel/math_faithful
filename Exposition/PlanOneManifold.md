## 1. What the existing Appendix actually gives

`Milnor/Appendix/OneManifold.lean` (1,704 lines) and `Milnor/Appendix/HalfSpace.lean` (74 lines) were read line by line. They prove the *consequence* the book uses, for *topological* one-manifolds, and they contain rather more reusable mathematics than their headline statement suggests.

**The setting.** A topological space `M` with charts to a model space `H` that is merely *homeomorphic* to the half-line `[0, ∞)` (the homeomorphism `φ` is an explicit argument, and `coord`/`ofCoord` turn every chart into a real coordinate). The boundary `B` is not defined but *assumed compatible*: `BoundaryCompatible` says every chart in the atlas sends exactly the points of `B` to coordinate `0`. This assumption is unavoidable topologically — it stands in for invariance of domain, which the file never proves. Hausdorffness (`T2Space`) and, for the counting theorems, `CompactSpace` are assumed.

**The central object.** `Arc M B` is a homeomorphism from an open subset of `M` onto a *bounded* order-connected set of reals with at least two points, under which `B` corresponds exactly to the non-interior points of the target. It carries: both maps, source and target, the two inverse laws, openness of the source, order-connectedness/nontriviality/boundedness of the target, continuity both ways, and the boundary law `x ∈ B ↔ toFun x ∉ interior target`. Derived API: injectivity both ways, images, relative openness of images, preconnectedness of the source, `interior target = Ioo (sInf) (sSup)`, the "a non-interior point of the target is the inf or the sup" lemma, and coordinate negation `Arc.neg`.

**Arcs from charts.** `arcOfChart` cuts a chart down to the preimage of a prescribed interval `J ⊆ [0,∞)`, with eight side conditions on `J`. Two instances: `exists_arc_of_mem_boundary` gives, at a boundary point, an arc with target `Ico 0 ε` and the point at `0`; `exists_arc_of_notMem_boundary` gives, at an interior point, an arc with open target. From these: boundary points are isolated (`boundary_inter_source_eq`), `B` is closed, and `B` is finite when `M` is compact (`finite_boundary`).

**Milnor's Lemma 1, in topological form (the `Hull`, `Monotone`, `Transition`, `TwoIntervals` and `Counting` sections).** This is the real content, about 650 lines:
* `hull S t₀ = {t | uIcc t t₀ ⊆ S}` is the connected component of `t₀` in `S`; hulls are order-connected, equal-or-disjoint, relatively open in the ambient interval, and their attained ends belong to them.
* `strictMonoOn_or_strictAntiOn_of_ordConnected`: a continuous injection on an order-connected set of reals is strictly monotone or strictly antitone. (Already reused by `Milnor/Chapter5/MonoAt.lean`.)
* Four limit lemmas (`tendsto_sSup_of_monotoneOn` and siblings) giving the one-sided limits of a monotone transition at an unattained end.
* `overlap A A'` (the `A`-coordinates of the common source) and `trans A A'` (the transition), with injectivity, continuity, relative openness of the overlap.
* `end_lemma`: if the transition has a limit `L` at an end of the overlap that is not itself in the overlap, then `L` is outside the target of `A'` — geometrically, the parametrization leaves `A'` there. With `lt_of_end_lemma_upper/lower` this says a component of the overlap that ends "inside `A`" must run off the top or the bottom of `A'`.
* `clash_upper`, `clash_lower`: two *different* components cannot both run off the top (or both off the bottom) of `A'`. `not_upper_of_sSup_mem`, `not_lower_of_sInf_mem`: a component cannot run off an end that `A'` actually attains.
* `end_right`, `end_left`: the dichotomy for one component, split by monotone/antitone.
* `overlap_ordConnected`: **the overlap is a single interval** — but only under the hypothesis that `A` starts at a boundary point with coordinate `0` and target `Ico 0 c`. That hypothesis enters exactly once, through `trans_zero_eq_sInf_or_sSup`, which says the image of the boundary endpoint is an endpoint of the other arc's target. This is where the file buys its simplification: the two-component (circle) case is excluded by fiat, not proved impossible.

**Extension and induction.** `exists_extension_aux` (≈370 lines) glues an arc `A` from a boundary point with an arbitrary arc `A'` that meets it, in the normalised position where all overlap coordinates lie below `d = A'.toFun x`. **The glue is affine**: the new coordinate is `A.toFun` on `A.source` and `c + (A'.toFun · − d)` elsewhere, with inverse `A.invFun` below `c` and `A'.invFun (d + (· − c))` above. All the work is continuity at the seam (via the one-sided limits), the shape of the new target, and the boundary law. `exists_extension` removes the normalisation using `Arc.neg`. `IsFrom A p` records "`p` is the left endpoint of `A`, at coordinate `0`, with target inside `[0,∞)`"; from it: the target is `Ico 0 c` or `Icc 0 c`; when the sup is attained the source is the whole connected component of `p` (compactness plus clopen-ness) and `B ∩ component p = {p, A.invFun (sSup)}`. `exists_closed_arc` runs a finite-cover induction (not Zorn) on the number of covering chart-arcs not yet absorbed. `exists_partner`, `even_ncard_of_involution` and `even_ncard_boundary` finish by parity of a fixed-point-free involution.

**The specialisation.** `HalfSpace.lean` transfers this to the project's own manifolds with boundary: `halfSpaceHomeomorph` identifies `HalfSpace Z` with `Ici 0` when `Z` is a subsingleton, `IsHalfSliceSubmanifold.boundaryCompatible` discharges the compatibility hypothesis from the definition of a half-slice chart (which demands it), and `IsHalfSliceSubmanifold.even_ncard_boundary` is the statement the book uses.

**What is *not* there:** no smoothness anywhere; no classification; no two-component case; no circle; no statement about the non-compact case; and `finite_boundary`/`even_ncard_boundary` need compactness (rightly — see §7 on the long line).

**The Appendix is load-bearing and cannot be replaced.** `Milnor/Chapter2/Brouwer.lean:218` and `Milnor/Chapter4/Homotopy.lean:157` use `IsHalfSliceSubmanifold.even_ncard_boundary`; `Milnor/Chapter5/LemmaOne.lean:79,92` uses `OneManifold.exists_closed_arc` and `finite_boundary`; `Milnor/Chapter5/ArcSign.lean:45,192` takes an `OneManifold.Arc` and an `IsFrom` as *data*, traversing the arc to compare signs at its two ends; `Milnor/Chapter5/MonoAt.lean:94,105` uses the monotonicity dichotomy. So the classification must be *added on top*: nothing in the present two files may be deleted or restated.

## 2. Mathlib audit: the earlier survey verified and corrected

Checked against the Mathlib copy in `.lake/packages/mathlib` (toolchain v4.33.1).

**Confirmed, and load-bearing.**
* Smooth partitions of unity, `Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/PartitionOfUnity.lean`. `SmoothPartitionOfUnity.exists_isSubordinate` (line 554) and `exists_isSubordinate_chartAt_source` (574) need `T2Space`, `SigmaCompactSpace`, `FiniteDimensional ℝ E`, `IsManifold I ∞ M` — and crucially **no boundarylessness**. Same for `SmoothBumpFunction` (`Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/BumpFunction.lean`). So the partition-of-unity technique is available on manifolds with boundary.
* **A find the survey missed, and the single most useful one:** `exists_contMDiffSection_forall_mem_convex_of_local` (same file, ≈line 591). Given a vector bundle over `M` and a *convex* subset of each fibre, plus local smooth sections landing in those subsets, it produces a *global* smooth section landing in them. This is precisely the engine for "a manifold admits a Riemannian metric" (positive-definite forms are convex in each fibre), and it works for an arbitrary model with corners. There is also a trivial-bundle version, `exists_contMDiffMap_forall_mem_convex_of_local`.
* `ModelWithCorners.isBoundaryPoint_iff_of_mem_atlas` and `isInteriorPoint_iff_of_mem_atlas`, `Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/IsManifold/InteriorBoundary.lean:305,315`. For `C¹` manifolds, being a boundary point can be tested in *any* chart of the atlas, not just the preferred one (the proof, lines 259–292, uses convexity and closedness of the model range and the invertibility of transition derivatives — Mathlib's own comment notes the topological analogue needs singular homology). **Consequence for us: in the smooth setting `BoundaryCompatible` is a theorem, not a hypothesis.** That alone is a cheap, genuinely useful addition to the existing Appendix.
* The model range is convex with nonempty interior (`convex_range'`, `nonempty_interior'`) and **closed** (`ModelWithCorners.isClosed_range`, `IsManifold/Basic.lean:398`). In dimension one this pins the model down: after a linear identification `E ≃L[ℝ] ℝ`, the range is a closed interval with interior, i.e. `ℝ`, a closed half-line, or a compact interval. Combined with `uniqueDiffOn_convex` (`Mathlib/Analysis/Calculus/TangentCone/Real.lean:79`) derivatives on the model range are unique, so `derivWithin` behaves at boundary points.
* Transition maps are automatically diffeomorphisms *both ways*: `contDiffGroupoid` is `Pregroupoid.groupoid (contDiffPregroupoid n I)` (`IsManifold/Basic.lean:636,675`), and membership gives smoothness of a chart transition **and of its inverse**. So in a metric-free route one never needs a one-sided inverse function theorem for transitions — they come with smooth inverses from the atlas.
* `IsLocalDiffeomorph.diffeomorphOfBijective` (`Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/LocalDiffeomorph.lean:355`): a bijective local diffeomorphism is a diffeomorphism — with **no** finite-dimensionality and **no** boundarylessness hypothesis. This is Milnor's repeated "an injective local diffeomorphism is a diffeomorphism onto its image". Also `Diffeomorph.image_boundary` / `preimage_boundary` (InteriorBoundary.lean:424–444) for transporting boundaries.
* Intervals as manifolds: `Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Instances/Real.lean` gives `Icc x y` (for `Fact (x < y)`) a two-chart structure on `EuclideanHalfSpace 1`, `IsManifold (𝓡∂ 1) n (Icc x y)`, and `boundary_Icc : (𝓡∂ 1).boundary (Icc x y) = {⊥, ⊤}`. `Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Instances/Icc.lean` gives the two bridges that make a diffeomorphism with `Icc 0 1` cheap to *construct*: `contMDiff_iff_comp_subtypeVal_Icc` (a map *into* `Icc` is smooth iff its real-valued composite is) and `contMDiffOn_comp_projIcc_iff` (a map *out of* `Icc` is smooth iff its composite with the projection `ℝ → Icc` is smooth on the interval). Neither needs `IsManifold` on the other side.
* The circle: `Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Instances/Sphere.lean:571,574` charts `Circle` on `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 1)` and makes it an analytic manifold and Lie group; `contMDiff_circleExp` (595) says `t ↦ exp(it)` is smooth; `mfderiv_coe_sphere_injective` (553) and `range_mfderiv_coe_sphere` (522) let one show that map is an immersion. `Mathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Complex/Circle.lean` supplies `Circle.exp_eq_exp`, `Circle.exp_eq_one`, `Circle.exp_arg`, `isLocalHomeomorph_circleExp` and `homeomorphCircle' : AddCircle (2π) ≃ₜ Circle`.
* Integral curves: `IsMIntegralCurve` and friends in `Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/IntegralCurve/`. Local existence `exists_isMIntegralCurveAt_of_contMDiffAt` needs `CompleteSpace E` and that the curve stays in the *interior*; uniqueness needs `T2Space`; `exists_isMIntegralCurve_of_isMIntegralCurveOn` (UniformTime.lean:162) upgrades a uniform existence time to global curves; `IsMIntegralCurve.periodic_xor_injective` (ExistUnique.lean:268) is the dichotomy.
* `Manifold.metrizableSpace` and `ChartedSpace.secondCountable_of_sigmaCompact` (`Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Metrizable.lean`): σ-compact plus Hausdorff plus finite-dimensional model gives second countable and metrizable. Compact manifolds get this free.
* No classification of one-manifolds anywhere in Mathlib, and no existence theorem for a Riemannian metric. Confirmed.

**Corrections to the survey.**
1. **`Mathlib/Analysis/ConstantSpeed.lean` is of no use for Milnor's route.** `HasUnitSpeedOn f s` is a statement about `eVariationOn`, the *metric* variation, not about derivatives; and `naturalParameterization f s a` is literally `f ∘ Function.invFunOn (variationOnFromTo f s a) s`, a choice-based set-theoretic inverse. The two theorems about it (`has_unit_speed_naturalParameterization`, `edist_naturalParameterization_eq_zero`) give unit *metric* speed and equality only up to `edist = 0`. Nothing says the reparametrisation is smooth, let alone a diffeomorphism. Milnor's Lemma 1 needs `f' ` and `g'` to be unit *vectors* so that the transition has derivative `±1`; that has to be built from scratch as `σ(u) = ∫ ‖h'‖`, with smoothness from the fundamental theorem of calculus.
2. **`Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Riemannian/` gives lengths, not parametrisations.** `Manifold.pathELength` and `riemannianEDist` are integrals of `‖mfderiv γ t 1‖` and infima over paths, with congruence, additivity and monotone-reparametrisation lemmas. Useful if one wanted the metric geometry; irrelevant to reparametrising by arc length.
3. **A Riemannian metric is bundle data with a side condition.** `ContMDiffRiemannianMetric IB n F E` (`Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/VectorBundle/Riemannian.lean`) requires, besides symmetry, positivity and smoothness, that `{v | ⟪v,v⟫ < 1}` be von Neumann bounded. For a finite-dimensional fibre this is automatic but must still be proved. The tangent bundle is a smooth vector bundle for *any* model with corners (`Tangent.lean:330`), so the boundary is not an obstacle.
4. **`periodic_xor_injective` assumes `BoundarylessManifold` and a *global* integral curve.** Both restrictions bite: the with-boundary case is outside its scope, and global existence has to be obtained from compactness plus a uniform existence time. Further, "periodic with some positive period" is weaker than what the circle case needs; one must still extract the *minimal* period (the period group is a closed subgroup of `ℝ`, discrete because the curve is locally injective) and then descend.
5. **No smooth structure on quotients.** `Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Instances/Quotient.lean` gives only a charted-space structure on a quotient by a free properly discontinuous action; making the quotient a smooth manifold and the projection smooth are listed TODOs. So `ℝ/Tℤ` is not available as a smooth manifold: the circle must be `Circle`.

**Small additional assets.** `Real.smoothTransition` (`Mathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/SmoothTransition.lean`), already used in `Milnor/Chapter8/LinkingSphere.lean`; `intervalIntegral.integral_hasDerivAt_right` and `contDiff_infty_iff_deriv` (`Mathlib/Analysis/Calculus/ContDiff/Deriv.lean:103`), from which "the primitive of a smooth function is smooth" is a twenty-line lemma; the project's own `Milnor.contDiff_intervalIntegral_infty` (`Milnor/Chapter6/Hadamard.lean`) as an alternative; `Opens M` is a manifold and a single open embedding into the model gives a one-chart manifold (`Topology.IsOpenEmbedding.isManifold_singleton`), so `Ici 0`, `Ico 0 1`, `Ioo a b` and `ℝ` are all cheap to set up as one-manifold models; `ContinuousLinearEquiv.ofFinrankEq` and `convex_iff_ordConnected` for the one-dimensional normalisation; the project's manifold inverse function theorem `Milnor.isLocalDiffeomorphAt_of_isInvertible_mfderiv` (`Milnor/Chapter1/InverseFunction.lean`), which does assume both models boundaryless.

## 3. The three routes compared

### (a) Milnor's own route: a metric, arc length, Lemma 1, a maximal parametrisation

*To build from scratch.* (i) A Riemannian metric on the manifold — or, in dimension one, the equivalent and lighter "length element". (ii) Arc-length reparametrisation of a chart: `σ(u) = ∫_{u₀}^u ρ`, smooth with positive derivative, and smoothness of `σ⁻¹` **up to a boundary endpoint** (the one-sided inverse function theorem, which Mathlib does not have; provable by bootstrapping `(σ⁻¹)' = 1/(σ' ∘ σ⁻¹)` on a set with unique derivatives, but it is real work). (iii) Milnor's Lemma 1 — with the large caveat below. (iv) Maximality.

*What carries weight.* The metric existence rides on `exists_contMDiffSection_forall_mem_convex_of_local`. The primitive's smoothness rides on the fundamental theorem of calculus plus `contDiff_infty_iff_deriv`. `IsLocalDiffeomorph.diffeomorphOfBijective` closes each case. **And, decisively, the hard half of Lemma 1 is already proved in the project**: the file's `end_lemma` / `clash_upper` / `clash_lower` / `end_right` / `end_left` machinery is exactly Milnor's "segments run edge to edge and at most one ends on each edge", in topological dress. Unit speed contributes only the *affine* form of the transition: both parametrisations have unit speed, so the transition has `|derivative| = 1`, hence derivative `±1`, hence constant sign on each component by continuity, hence `τ(s) = ±s + c`. That is a hundred lines, not a thousand.

*Where the boundary enters.* Three places. A parametrisation whose domain has an endpoint sends it to a boundary point; arc length must be integrated on a half-open interval and inverted there; and the "two-component" case is impossible once an arc starts at a boundary point (already the content of `overlap_ordConnected`).

*What could go wrong.* The metric stage is the one with real uncertainty — either the bundle-of-bilinear-forms plumbing (Hom bundles, the von Neumann bounded condition, smoothness of a local metric pulled back through a chart) or, in the lighter version, the bookkeeping of a locally finite sum of absolute values of chart derivatives. The one-sided smooth inverse is a second soft spot. Against that: **the affine glue means the existing 370-line extension construction survives essentially verbatim** — its formula `c + (A'.toFun · − d)` *is* the unit-speed transition, so near the seam the two branches are literally the same function and smoothness of the glued arc is almost free.

### (b) The metric-free route: monotone transitions, a maximal chain of charts

*To build from scratch.* Charts of a one-manifold give homeomorphisms onto intervals directly (in this project even more directly: a half-slice chart's first coordinate *is* the real parameter), and transitions are strictly monotone with smooth inverses for free from the atlas. What is missing is the **smooth glue**: to merge two overlapping parametrisations into one you must choose a common parameter, and an arbitrary monotone transition is not affine, so the piecewise-defined map has a kink at the seam. The fix is a *smooth monotone extension* lemma: given a smooth increasing `τ` on `[t₁, c)`, produce a smooth increasing `Θ` on `[t₁, ∞)` agreeing with `τ` near `t₁`, by surgery on the derivative — `Θ(t) = τ(t₁) + ∫_{t₁}^t (χ τ' + (1-χ))` with `χ` a `Real.smoothTransition` bump equal to `1` near `t₁` and `0` further along. Positivity of the integrand gives monotonicity; the primitive is smooth; agreement near `t₁` gives smoothness at the seam. Cost: roughly 200 lines, and no metric at all.

*What carries weight.* Everything topological in the existing file, unchanged, including the fact that the overlap is a single interval and the finite-cover induction. Mathlib's `Icc` bridges for the final diffeomorphism.

*Where the boundary enters.* Only at the starting point of the arc, exactly as now.

*What could go wrong — and this is the decisive defect.* **The extension is not an extension.** The glued arc agrees with the old one only on an initial piece; past the seam the parameter has been changed. Three consequences. First, no Zorn: the poset of arcs ordered by restriction has no useful chains, so the non-compact case cannot be done by a maximal parametrisation and needs a countable exhaustion with a coherent choice of seams — a delicate diagonal argument. Second, the circle case is out of reach: closing up requires the two gluings, at the two ends, to produce *exactly* the same period, and the freedom to reparametrise has already been spent at the first seam. Third, one cannot even ask whether the transition is affine, so Milnor's picture of segments of slope `±1` has no counterpart. Verdict: route (b) is the cheapest way to get the *compact with boundary* case (about 750 lines less than route (a), since neither metric nor arc length is needed), and it is a dead end for the circle and for the non-compact case.

### (c) The flow route: a nowhere-zero field, integral curves, the periodic-or-injective dichotomy

*To build from scratch.* A nowhere-vanishing smooth vector field; the flow's completeness (uniform existence time on a compact manifold); extraction of the minimal period; the descent of a periodic curve to `Circle`; and, for the injective alternative, the identification of the image.

*What carries weight.* `IsMIntegralCurve.periodic_xor_injective` is genuinely Milnor's dichotomy, and uniqueness of integral curves replaces Lemma 1 altogether — a real saving. `exists_isMIntegralCurve_of_isMIntegralCurveOn` converts a uniform local existence time into global curves.

*Where the boundary enters — fatally.* Mathlib's existence, uniqueness and dichotomy theorems all assume `BoundarylessManifold` (existence needs the curve to stay in the interior, which is false at a boundary point, and Mathlib flags the boundary case as an explicit TODO citing Lee's Theorem 9.34). So the route says nothing about manifolds with boundary — which is the case the book actually uses.

*Is the circularity real?* Yes, and it must be stated plainly. A nowhere-zero vector field on a one-manifold is the same thing as an orientation, and the usual proof that every connected one-manifold is orientable *is* the classification. Neither Mathlib nor this project has orientability of a manifold in any form (see `Leftovers.md` item 6a, where it is separately planned). So starting route (c) from "take a nowhere-zero field" assumes what is to be proved.

*How to break the circle honestly.* Three options. (1) Replace the field by a **metric**: a Riemannian metric needs no orientation, and a unit-speed curve is determined once a direction is chosen at one point; but then the equation `γ' = v(γ)` is no longer autonomous and Mathlib's uniqueness theorem does not apply — one is back to route (a) with extra steps. (2) Run route (a) or (b) first and harvest the field afterwards: at the moment the extension gets stuck with a two-component overlap, the two components have the *same* slope (Milnor's Step 5), which is exactly the statement that the two local directions agree, so the field can be glued from the arc's velocity and the last chart's by one bump function. This is not circular, but it makes (c) a corollary rather than a route. (3) Restrict the statement to *orientable* one-manifolds, i.e. assume the field. Conclusion: (c) is not a viable primary route; option (2) makes it a cheap *alternative* proof of the compact boundaryless case once the rest is in place, and I recommend keeping it in reserve rather than in the plan.

## 4. Recommendation

**Route (a), Milnor's own, built on top of the existing topological Appendix rather than beside it.**

Three reasons. First, the existing file already contains the hard, topological half of Milnor's Lemma 1; unit speed adds only the affine form of the transition, so the expensive-looking Lemma 1 is the cheapest stage of the plan. Second, the existing extension construction already glues with an affine change of coordinate, so with unit-speed arcs the two branches agree near the seam and the smoothness fields are nearly free — the 370 lines that dominate the existing file are reused, not rewritten. Third, only affine transitions make the extension a *genuine* extension, and that is what unlocks both a maximality argument for the non-compact case and the exact period needed for the circle. The price is the metric, which is worth paying once: an existence theorem for a Riemannian metric (or its one-dimensional shadow) is the only piece of general theory the project would gain, and it is Mathlib-worthy on its own.

Keep route (b)'s derivative-surgery lemma in the toolbox anyway: it is what constructs the smooth monotone bumps used for the metric's positivity and for the reserve construction of §3(c)(2). And record route (b) as the answer if the scope is ever cut to "compact with boundary only".

## 5. The staged plan

All new files under `Milnor/Appendix/Smooth/`. Master hypotheses, fixed once in stage 0 and used throughout: a real normed space `E` with `finrank ℝ E = 1`, a model with corners `I : ModelWithCorners ℝ E H`, and a Hausdorff smooth manifold `M` charted on `H` with `IsManifold I ∞ M`. The boundary is Mathlib's `I.boundary M`; no compatibility hypothesis is assumed. Compactness or σ-compactness is added only where stated.

### Stage 0 — `Setup.lean`: the one-dimensional model, and the boundary for free
*Proves.* A linear identification `E ≃L[ℝ] ℝ`; the image of the model range is a **closed** interval with nonempty interior, hence `ℝ`, a closed half-line, or a compact interval, and derivatives on it are unique. A real coordinate attached to every chart. The chart-independence of boundary points, imported from Mathlib, in the form the project uses: *for every chart of the atlas and every point of its source, the point is a boundary point exactly when its coordinate is an endpoint of the model range.* Two normalised local models: every boundary point has a chart-derived partial homeomorphism onto `Ico 0 ε` sending it to `0`, smooth both ways; every interior point one onto `Ioo (−ε) ε`. As a by-product, **`Milnor.OneManifold.BoundaryCompatible` is discharged for any smooth one-manifold with boundary whose model range is a half-line**, so the existing counting theorem applies to smooth one-manifolds with no side condition.
*Depends on.* Mathlib only.
*Risks.* Low to medium. The compact-interval model range (a "one-manifold with corners") must be allowed; charts at interior points may have to be shrunk away from the model's own endpoints. The sign of the identification `E ≃ ℝ` must be handled so that the half-line is `[0,∞)` and not `(−∞,0]`.
*Estimate.* 350 lines.

### Stage 1 — `SmoothArc.lean`: smooth arcs as an extension of the existing structure
*Proves.* `SmoothArc` = an `OneManifold.Arc M (I.boundary M)` together with smoothness of both maps (the forward map smooth on the source as a map of manifolds; the inverse smooth on the target as a map from the interval). Derived: the chain rule on the target forces both derivatives to be nonzero, so a smooth arc is an immersion and its inverse transition is smooth; negation of the coordinate; construction of smooth arcs from the two normalised local models of stage 0.
*Statements.* `SmoothArc.derivWithin_ne_zero`; `SmoothArc.neg`; `exists_smoothArc_of_mem_boundary` and `exists_smoothArc_of_notMem_boundary`, mirroring the existing `exists_arc_of_…` pair.
*Depends on.* Stage 0; the whole `Arc` API of the existing file, inherited free of charge.
*Risks.* Low. The only subtlety is that smoothness on a half-open target is Whitney-style smoothness on a non-open set; unique derivatives from stage 0 make the chain and quotient rules usable there.
*Estimate.* 450 lines.

### Stage 2 — `TwoComponents.lean`: Milnor's Lemma 1, topological half, in general
*Proves.* For any two arcs in a Hausdorff space (no smoothness, no boundary hypothesis): **the overlap has at most two components**; if it has an "inner" component, that component is the only one and its transition image is the whole target of the other arc; if it has two, each of them runs out to an end of the first arc's target.
*How.* Purely from the existing lemmas. A component with both ends interior runs off both the top and the bottom of the other arc's target, so its image is the whole target and, by injectivity, no other component can exist. Otherwise every component touches an end of the ambient target, and there are at most two ends. `clash_upper` and `clash_lower` supply the contradictions; `not_upper_of_sSup_mem` and `not_lower_of_sInf_mem` handle attained ends.
*Depends on.* The existing `Hull`, `Transition`, `TwoIntervals`, `Counting` sections only. Independent of stages 0 and 1, and of the choice of route.
*Risks.* Medium. The hull bookkeeping ("at most one inner hull", "an inner hull is the only hull") is fiddly, but every ingredient already exists and is stated in exactly the needed form. The existing `overlap_ordConnected` becomes the special case where the arc starts at a boundary point and is kept as is.
*Estimate.* 300 lines. **This stage is worth doing first: it is the only one that is pure profit regardless of which route is chosen, and it is the statement the Comparison edition can already quote as "Milnor's Lemma of the Appendix".**

### Stage 3 — `LengthElement.lean`: the metric
*Proves.* Existence of a global smooth length element: a family of positive smooth functions `ρ_e`, one per chart, on the chart's coordinate image, satisfying the cocycle rule `ρ_e(u) = ρ_{e'}(τ(u)) · |τ'(u)|`. Constructed as `ρ_e(u) = Σ_i λ_i(e⁻¹ u) · |(φ_i ∘ e⁻¹)'(u)|` for a smooth partition of unity subordinate to the chart domains; each summand is smooth because the transition derivative never vanishes, so its absolute value is smooth where the bump is supported, and extends by zero outside.
*Depends on.* Stage 0; `SmoothPartitionOfUnity.exists_isSubordinate_chartAt_source`. Needs `SigmaCompactSpace M` (automatic when compact) and finite-dimensionality of `E` (automatic from `finrank = 1`).
*Risks.* **Highest of the plan.** Locally finite sums of smooth functions on a manifold; smoothness of the absolute value via non-vanishing on the support; the cocycle identity by the chain rule. A blunter alternative if this bogs down: restrict to the compact case, where the cover is finite and the sum is a finite sum.
*Optional upgrade, recommended as a separate deliverable.* Build a genuine `ContMDiffRiemannianMetric` on the tangent bundle by feeding the pull-back of an inner product through each chart into `exists_contMDiffSection_forall_mem_convex_of_local`. This is the Mathlib-worthy statement ("every σ-compact Hausdorff finite-dimensional manifold, with or without boundary, admits a smooth Riemannian metric"), it is strictly more work (Hom bundles, the von Neumann bounded side condition, smoothness of the local pull-back as a bundle section), and the classification does not need it. Put it in its own file, `RiemannianExists.lean`, 500 lines, and do not block on it.
*Estimate.* 450 lines for the length element.

### Stage 4 — `ArcLength.lean`: unit-speed arcs exist
*Proves.* `UnitSpeed A`: a smooth arc whose inverse has length-element speed one. Every point of `M` lies in the source of a unit-speed arc, with target `Ico 0 ε` at a boundary point and `Ioo (−ε) ε` at an interior point. Proof: reparametrise the stage-1 arc by `σ(u) = ∫_{u₀}^u ρ`; `σ` is smooth (the primitive of a smooth function, from the fundamental theorem of calculus and the characterisation of smoothness by derivatives), strictly increasing with positive derivative, and `σ⁻¹` is smooth — including at a boundary endpoint — by bootstrapping `(σ⁻¹)' = 1/(σ' ∘ σ⁻¹)` on a set with unique derivatives.
*Depends on.* Stages 1 and 3.
*Risks.* Medium-high, concentrated in the one-sided smooth inverse. Fallback if that fights back: extend `ρ` smoothly across the endpoint by the derivative-surgery trick of route (b) and invert on an open interval instead.
*Estimate.* 500 lines.
*This is Milnor's Lemma 1.1 (p. 54), the one his text disposes of with "by straightforward change of variables".*

### Stage 5 — `Affine.lean`: the transition of two unit-speed arcs
*Proves.* The transition between two unit-speed arcs has `|derivative| = 1` on the overlap, hence derivative `±1`, hence, on each component, `τ(s) = ±s + c`. Combined with stage 2: **Milnor's Lemma in full.** Corollary, and the point of the whole design: the existing `exists_extension` produces, from unit-speed arcs, a *unit-speed* arc that genuinely *extends* the first one — because on the overlap the second arc's coordinate is already `d + (· − c)`, so the piecewise formula is locally one smooth branch.
*Depends on.* Stages 2 and 4; the existing `exists_extension_aux` / `exists_extension`, whose statements are re-proved with the smoothness fields added.
*Risks.* Low to medium. The re-proof copies the existing structure; the new work is the three smoothness obligations and the observation that the seam is not a seam.
*Estimate.* 300 lines (plus 150 of re-stated extension).

### Stage 6 — `Compact.lean`: the compact case with boundary
*Proves.* A compact connected one-manifold with a boundary point is diffeomorphic to `Icc 0 1`; its boundary has exactly two points. Proof: the existing finite-cover induction (`exists_closed_arc`), now with unit-speed arcs, gives a unit-speed arc from a boundary point whose target is `Icc 0 L` and whose source is the whole component; rescale to `Icc 0 1`; convert to a `Diffeomorph` using Mathlib's two `Icc` bridges (a map out of `Icc` is smooth iff its composite with the projection from `ℝ` is; a map into `Icc` iff its real composite is).
*Depends on.* Stages 1, 4, 5; the existing `IsFrom` API and `exists_closed_arc`.
*Risks.* Low-medium; mostly transporting between "smooth on an interval of `ℝ`" and "smooth on the manifold `Icc 0 1`".
*Estimate.* 400 lines.
*After this stage the book's own use of the Appendix is fully covered, in a strictly stronger form than the present file (components identified, not just counted).*

### Stage 7 — `Circle.lean`: the compact boundaryless case
*Proves.* A compact connected one-manifold without boundary is diffeomorphic to `Circle`. Proof: with no boundary point to start from, the induction must at some stage meet a chart whose overlap with the current arc has two components (stage 2); the two components have the same slope (else the transitions would cross), which yields a period `T > 0` and a `T`-periodic unit-speed map `ℝ → M`, injective on a half-open period and a local diffeomorphism. Descend through `Circle.exp` rescaled to period `T`: that map is smooth, surjective, and a local diffeomorphism (its derivative is nonzero because the inclusion of the sphere into `ℂ` is an immersion and `d/dt e^{it} ≠ 0`; then the project's manifold inverse function theorem applies, both models being boundaryless). The induced map from `Circle` is a bijective local diffeomorphism, hence a diffeomorphism.
*Depends on.* Stages 2, 4, 5; `contMDiff_circleExp`, `mfderiv_coe_sphere_injective`, `Circle.exp_eq_exp`, `Milnor.isLocalDiffeomorphAt_of_isInvertible_mfderiv`, `IsLocalDiffeomorph.diffeomorphOfBijective`.
*Risks.* **High.** Three sub-problems: extracting the minimal period (the period set is a closed subgroup of `ℝ`, discrete by local injectivity, hence generated by a smallest positive element); well-definedness and smoothness of the descended map, which must be obtained through local inverses of `Circle.exp` rather than any quotient-manifold theory (Mathlib has none); and the two-component geometry itself, which is the one part of Milnor's Step 5 with no counterpart in the existing file.
*Estimate.* 550 lines.

### Stage 8 — `NonCompact.lean`: the remaining two cases
*Proves.* A connected, σ-compact one-manifold that is not compact is diffeomorphic to `Ici 0` if it has a boundary point and to `ℝ` if it does not. Proof: because extensions are now genuine extensions, the union of a chain of unit-speed arcs extending a fixed one is again such an arc, so a maximal one exists; if its image were a proper open subset it would have a limit point outside, and a unit-speed arc there would extend it (stage 5) — so it is onto. Then rescale: every open interval is diffeomorphic to `ℝ` and every half-open one to `Ici 0`. Charted-space structures for `Ici 0`, `Ico 0 1`, `Ioo a b` come from a single open embedding into the model space.
*Depends on.* Stages 3–5.
*Risks.* Medium. Two specific traps: the existing `Arc` requires a *bounded* target, which a maximal arc need not have, so either the boundedness field is relaxed for this stage or the chain is renormalised at the end; and the chain argument must be set up as a Zorn argument on a genuine set of functions.
*Estimate.* 600 lines.

### Stage 9 — `Main.lean`: assembly and the project's own vocabulary
*Proves.* The Appendix theorem: a connected σ-compact Hausdorff smooth one-manifold, with or without boundary, is diffeomorphic to exactly one of `Circle`, `Icc 0 1`, `Ici 0`, `ℝ`. Plus the specialisation to the project's own manifolds with boundary — for a compact half-slice submanifold over a zero-dimensional `Z`, every connected component is diffeomorphic to `Circle` or to `Icc 0 1`, and the boundary has an even number of points, with the pairing realised by the two endpoints of a component.
*Depends on.* Stages 6–8; `Milnor/Chapter2/Boundary.lean`; the existing `HalfSpace.lean` as the template.
*Risks.* Low.
*Estimate.* 250 lines.

## 6. The final theorem, in the project's vocabulary

Two forms should be stated, matching the two idioms the project already uses.

**Abstract form** (the primary one, and the one that could go to Mathlib). Quantify over a real normed space `E` of dimension one, a model with corners over it, and a Hausdorff smooth manifold charted on that model, with `σ`-compactness; conclude a diffeomorphism with one of the four models `Circle`, `Icc 0 1`, `Ici 0`, `ℝ`, each with its own model with corners (`𝓡 1` for the circle, `𝓡∂ 1` for the two half-line-type intervals, `𝓘(ℝ, ℝ)` for `ℝ`). Mathlib's `Diffeomorph` already takes two different models, so no translation layer is needed. The boundary is `I.boundary M`, with no compatibility hypothesis: stage 0 proves it.

This choice is deliberate and differs from the existing topological Appendix, which fixes a homeomorphism of the model space with `[0,∞)`. In the smooth setting the model must be a model with corners, so the right hypothesis is "the model space is one-dimensional", and the three possible model ranges (line, closed half-line, compact interval) are handled uniformly by stage 0. It also differs from the rest of the project, which usually works with slice submanifolds of an ambient manifold — but here the abstract form is the natural one, because the theorem is about the intrinsic structure, and the slice form follows.

**Slice form** (the one the book uses, and the one downstream files can consume). For a compact half-slice submanifold `X` of an ambient manifold, with boundary `B`, modelled on the half-space over a `Z` with one point — that is, a compact one-manifold with boundary in the sense of Chapter 2 — every connected component of `X` is diffeomorphic to `Circle` or to `Icc 0 1`, the components with boundary are exactly those diffeomorphic to `Icc 0 1`, each contributes exactly two boundary points, and `B` is finite of even cardinality. This is the existing `IsHalfSliceSubmanifold.even_ncard_boundary` with the decomposition added, and it should be proved by transporting the abstract theorem exactly as `HalfSpace.lean` transports the topological one.

## 7. Reuse and supersession, and what must be said honestly

**Reused verbatim, no change.** The whole `Arc` structure and its API; `hull` and its eight lemmas; `strictMonoOn_or_strictAntiOn_of_ordConnected` and the four one-sided limit lemmas; `overlap`, `trans` and their API; `end_lemma` and its two corollaries; `inter_nonempty_of_sSup_eq` / `sInf_eq`; `clash_upper`, `clash_lower`, `not_upper_of_sSup_mem`, `not_lower_of_sInf_mem`, `end_right`, `end_left`; `overlap_ordConnected`; `IsFrom` and all five of its lemmas; `exists_mem_closure_of_ne`; the finite-cover induction of `exists_closed_arc`; `exists_partner`; `even_ncard_of_involution`. That is the great majority of the 1,704 lines.

**Reused with smoothness fields added, structure untouched.** `arcOfChart` and the two `exists_arc_of_…` lemmas (stage 1); `exists_extension_aux` and `exists_extension` (stage 5). The affine glue in the existing construction is exactly the unit-speed transition, which is why this is addition and not rewriting.

**Logically subsumed but which must be kept.** `finite_boundary` and `even_ncard_boundary`, and their slice specialisation, are consequences of the classification — but they are cited by `Milnor/Chapter2/Brouwer.lean`, `Milnor/Chapter4/Homotopy.lean` and `Milnor/Chapter5/LemmaOne.lean`, and `Milnor/Chapter5/ArcSign.lean` consumes `Arc` and `IsFrom` as data. **Nothing may be deleted.** The classification should be an additional file importing the present ones, and the only edit worth making to existing files is a docstring pointer. If one day someone wants to re-derive the parity from the classification, that is a separate refactor with a real cost in Chapter 5.

**Genuinely superseded (in content, not in the file).** The Appendix's own caveat — "the two-component case never occurs because we always start at a boundary point" — is removed by stage 2, and the note in `Exposition/Appendix.md` §5 and in the Comparison row G.5 ("the classification is not stated") will need rewriting.

**Things that must be said plainly in the write-up.**
1. **The classification is false without a countability hypothesis.** The long line is a connected Hausdorff smooth one-manifold, not second countable, and diffeomorphic to neither the circle nor any interval. Milnor's manifolds sit in `ℝᵏ` and are second countable for free; his proof uses this silently, twice — the Euclidean metric he restricts to `M` is a Riemannian metric, and a manifold admitting one is metrizable. So the non-compact statement must assume σ-compactness (or second countability, or paracompactness). In the *compact* case nothing extra is needed: compact plus Hausdorff plus a finite-dimensional model gives second countable by Mathlib's own `ChartedSpace.secondCountable_of_sigmaCompact`. This belongs in the list of hidden hypotheses the project keeps.
2. **The existence of a Riemannian metric is the one piece of real general theory the Appendix needs, and Mathlib does not have it.** Milnor never mentions it because his manifolds are embedded. This is precisely parallel to `Leftovers.md` item 1, where an inner product on the ambient space had to be added for the same reason.
3. **Smoothness makes the boundary well defined**, through Mathlib's chart-independence theorem for `C¹` manifolds. The topological Appendix had to assume it. Chapter 2's design note — that half-slice charts build the compatibility into the definition so that invariance of domain is never needed — can be strengthened: for smooth manifolds it is now a theorem.
4. **Milnor's "maximal parametrisation" really does need arc length**, not just any parametrisation: without the affine transitions, an extension is not an extension, the chain argument collapses, and the circle case loses its exact period. That is the sharp reason his Lemma 1 is stated for arc-length parametrisations and not for arbitrary charts, and it is worth a line in the prose, since the text does not say it.
5. **Mathlib's arc-length file does not do arc length in the sense needed.** `naturalParameterization` is a set-theoretic inverse of the total variation; it has unit metric speed and no smoothness. A reader of the earlier survey could easily believe otherwise.
6. **The flow route is circular as usually stated**, because a nowhere-zero field on a one-manifold is an orientation and the standard proof that one exists is the classification. The plan does not use it; if it is ever wanted, the non-circular order is: prove the classification first, then read off the field — at which point the two-component analysis of stage 2 has already supplied the fact that the two local directions agree, which is orientability.
